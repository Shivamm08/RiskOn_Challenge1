Background:

As part of the international efforts to curb greenhouse gas emissions (as established in the Paris Climate Accords) and transition to a green and sustainable economy, the European Commission launched the European Green Deal which is a set of policy initiatives with the overarching aim of making the European Union climate neutral by 2050.

As part of the European Green Deal various industry-specific sub-initiatives were launched focusing their efforts. For the financial industry these efforts are comprised in the “Sustainable Finance Action Plan” (SFAP). The SFAP establishes 10 overarching goals for the financial industry. Regulations in focus:

For the purposes of the Sustainable Finance program at JB there are 3 regulations which will be highlighted in this section as they directly affect how the JB ESG Methodology is built up:

1. Adaptions to the MiFID II Regulations:

The MiFID II regulation was amended such that it now requires financial service providers to ask their clients for their ESG preferences when investing. These ESG preferences must be gathered along three criteria, namely:

a) The proportion of investments aligned with the sustainability-related financial disclosure (SFDR) regulation of the EU

b) The alignment with the EU Taxonomy; and

c) The consideration of Principal Adverse Impact in their investments

2. The Sustainable Financial Disclosure Regulation (SFDR)

The SFDR regulation requires us to classify certain financial products (e.g. discretionary mandates and funds) based on their level of sustainability, taking into consideration both positive and negative impact factors on the environment, society and employee matters. These negative impacts are referred to as Principle Adverse Impacts (PAI). Furthermore, SFDR requires increasing disclosure of ESG factors the higher a financial product is classified.
