2. Description of the alert

Issuer Concentration describes the exposure of a client portfolio towards a single issuer having unprotected issuer risks (in the unlikely case of a default of the issuer) - notably fixed income, equity, structured products and warrants. This also includes the scenario that JB is the issuer. Mutual funds and exchange traded derivatives (ETDs) are excluded, because they are protected or guaranteed. Cash positions are also excluded (except Call/Time deposits). The default issuer concentration threshold is 20% per issuer in line with industry standards. In BC CH it is possible to amend the threshold upon client's own request.

Amongst others, the following lists some of the reasons why the alert also covers the scenario when JB is the issuer: 

  • Regulatory and contractual obligation to protect the client: the issuer concentration risk applies regardless of who the issuer is. For example in Switzerland FINMA explicitly states, that the bank shall treat own products also in terms of concentration and issuer not differently to third party issued products  
  • Transparency duty 
  • Conflict of interest if the bank were to exclude its own products from risk warnings, this would create a conflict of interest
  • Reputational risk


3. What triggers this alert?

An Issuer Concentration Risk alert can be triggered either:

a) in the Advisory Session as a result of a newly proposed transaction that causes the concentration for a single issuer in relation to the portfolio to exceed the defined maximum threshold or

b) in the Client Book as a result of overnight portfolio risk monitoring either when

    • there is a breach of the official threshold for 5 consecutive days or
    • immediately if the breach is significant: Value is more than 1% above the threshold

=> The alert is designed to tolerate minor exceedances as long as these are not persistent. 

In case of a custom instrument, the issuer concentration risk is not automatically calculated in Wealth Navigator for BC CH. This needs to be done manually.


4. How can this alert be resolved?

You can resolve the alert with one of the following actions:

A. Advisory Session (session alert)

Solicitation TypeDutyDocumentation (Justification / Contact Note)

Actively solicited

RM/IA must reduce the investment amount in the transaction or in other positions with the same issuer until the alert is not triggered anymore to make the investment advice suitable

or

RM/IA must not proceed with the transaction as long as the alert is shown, because the investment advice is unsuitable


Reverse solicited

RM/IA must advise against the transaction before its execution if it results in an Issuer Concentration Risk breach

or

RM/IA must advice to reduce investment amount or other positions with the same issuer

If client accepts the risk and wants to proceed:

RM/IA to document the client’s decision and rationale in the alert justification/ contact note as it is described here: →Justification rationale (dropdown values) for "client accepts risk"


Unsolicited

RM/IA must warn the order giver of the Issuer Concentration Risk breach before executing the transaction

If client accepts the risk and wants to proceed:

RM/IA to document the warning in the alert justification/contact note as it is described here: →Justification rationale (dropdown values) for "client accepts risk"

Important: In the event of any data issues in the system or in dedicated permitted use cases (e.g. workarounds) it is possible to overrule the alert and document it according to the defined process →Hard Block Override Procedure


B. Client Book (overnight portfolio monitoring alert)

DutyResolving the alert & Documentation (Resolution / Contact Note)

RM/IA must inform the client within 5 business days


RM/IA to create an investment proposal to reduce the position(s) leading to an Issuer Concentration Risk (i.e. reduce one or several positions with the same issuer) and provide it to the client. Use resolution note “investment proposal sent” (alert will be snoozed for 30 days)

If the client does not want to reduce the issuer concentration below the threshold and accepts the risk:

RM/IA to create a resolution note in the client book (“client accepts risk” – also see examples below).

Please note: In case of a single resolution note (use the check Action symbol [  ] in the line of the respective alert), the justification rationale are available to support documentation (→Justification rationale (dropdown values) for "client accepts risk"). However, if you are using “justify all” to close one or several alerts with “client accepts risk”, please note 1) the risks that have been explained, 2) the client’s decision after the explanation and 3) the client's rationale in the contact note.

If the client’s portfolio is enabled for One-way Notification (OWN):

RM/IA to create a resolution note in the client book/portfolio alert overview. Select justification option “client informed” and follow the process in Wealth Navigator to dispatch the portfolio monitoring notification to the client. Further details can be found under →One-way notification for monitoring alerts

If the client expresses a specific need, the RM/IA can also increase the Issuer Concentration Risk threshold in the portfolio configuration by following the guiding principles (→Portfolio Configuration) and create a contact note

If the client could not be reached, RM/IA to select “client not reached” in the alert justification and document 1) the date when the client was approached and 2) the channel in the contact note.


Validity of Resolution Notes (RN)

The Resolution Note is valid until 

  • the snooze period ends or
  • the situation “worsens” or “improves” during the lifetime of the RN (→Worsening/Improvement Concept)
  • if a new transaction leads to an increase in concentration

If the alert is retriggered during the snooze period, the client must be contacted and informed again about the new situation. Reference to the first contact is not sufficient.


5. Justification Examples

Use case

Examples 

Overnight portfolio monitoring alert

Example 1: 

  • I contacted the account holder as her portfolio has a 22% exposure to bonds issued by Boeing Co. I informed her of the risk that this concentration entails and recommended to sell the bond with the longest time to maturity.
  • She wanted to hold the positions against my advice and said she is comfortable with the risk. She asked if it would be possible not to be notified of concentrations below 30%. I said that the threshold can be changed to 30% upon her request and she asked me to do so.

Example 2:

  • I notified the account holder by email that his portfolio has a 30% concentration to Structured Products issued by BNP Paribas. These positions have been transferred to us from his previous bank. The client is very self-directed, so I asked what he wants to do and did not provide advice on any of the instruments.
  • The account holder decided to sell one position, which decreased the exposure to 25%. He informed me he wants to hold the remaining ones until maturity. He agreed to review again in 12 months.

Changes in mandate configuration

Example 1:

  • As documented in the resolution note on [date, time], it was agreed with the account holder to increase the issuer concentration risk threshold to 30%
  • The account holder also acknowledged that the change will apply to all issuers going forward
  • Rationale: she informed me that she is comfortable with higher concentrations to single issuers and does not want to be informed if a concentration is 30% or less

Example 2:

  • The account holder wants to decrease the risk in his portfolio by rotating from equities and corporate bonds into US treasury bills, notes and bonds and therefore have a 30% concentration in one issuer
  • I informed the account holder about the issuer concentration threshold of 20% and he requested that I change it to 30%. I informed him that this will apply to all issuers, which he acknowledged
  • Rationale: Client believes that risk assets will go down in the short term. He considers US treasuries to be free from default risk
