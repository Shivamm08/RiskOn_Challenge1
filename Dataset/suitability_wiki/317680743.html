Section I – Basic concepts

1. Who is the client?

The client is any individual or legal entity to whom Bank Julius Baer Europe ("BJBE") provides investment or ancillary services.

  • Where the account holder is an individual or legal entity with legal personality, the client is the account holder.
  • Where the account holder is a legal entity without legal personality, the client is whichever of the account participants BJBE is providing investment and ancillary services to. For example, where the account holder is a trust, the trustee is the client; where the account holder is a partnership, some or all partners are the client; etc. Legal and Compliance may be contacted for help to determine which account participant(s) must be considered as the client in this case.

2. What categories can the client have?

Under MiFID II, a client can be categorized as retail, elective professional, per se professional or eligible counterparty, the first having the most MiFID II protection and the latter having the least MiFID II protection.

Additionally, elective professional clients can be so for all asset classes or only for alternative investment funds and subordinated capital products (while remaining retail for all asset classes).

3. How do accounts with one client operate?

The way an account with one client operates and the level of investor protection it is afforded to it (in terms of appropriateness testing, suitability testing and assurance, product distribution rules, client reporting and best execution coverage) depends on the category of that single client.

For example:

  • An account is participated by a son (as account holder) and his father (as PoA holder), so it is the category of the son that determines this.
  • An account is participated by a legal entity with legal personality (as account holder) and 3 of its directors (as authorized representatives), so it is the category of the legal entity that determines this.
  • An account is participated by an individual (as account holder) and an EAM managing his portfolio (as PoA holder), so it is the category of the individual that determines this.
  • An account is participated by a trust (as account holder), the trustor (as trustor) a trustee (as PoA holder) and the 5 beneficiaries (as beneficiaries), so it is the category of the trustee that determines this.

4. How do accounts with multiple clients operate?

The way an account with multiple clients operates and the level of investor protection it is afforded to it (in terms of appropriateness testing, suitability testing and assurance, product distribution rules, client reporting and best execution coverage) depends on the category of the "weakest" or "most protected" client.

For example, an account is participated by two joint account holders, a husband categorized as elective professional and a wife categorized as retail, so it is the category of the wife that determines this. 

5. Is client category RID-related or CID-related?

At BJBE, client category is RID-related (i.e. related to account participants), not CID-related (i.e. not related to accounts).

RID = account participant

CID = account

7. Retail clients

7.1. When are clients categorized as retail?

BJBE categorizes all clients as retail by default at onboarding.

However, individual and legal entity retail clients may request to:

  • Opt up totally to elective professional (please refer to ), or
  • Opt up partially to elective professional for subordinated capital products (like CoCo bonds) or for alternative investment funds (please refer to ).

Opt-up requests can only - strictly - be made by the client on own initiative.

Additionally, legal entity retail clients may request to be re-categorized to per se professional or eligible counterparty on own initiative.

7.2. What knowledge and experience are retail clients presumed to have?

Retail clients are presumed to have the least knowledge and experience to understand investment risks and make investment decisions, which is why they are the most protected category.

7.3. What product restrictions do retail clients face?

Retail clients face product restrictions as per:

  • The target market defined by the manufacturer,
  • The law (e.g. CoCo bonds), and/or
  • BJBE's internal policy framework.

7.4. What information must retail clients receive?

Retail clients receive the most information pre-trade with a clear indication of investment risks (e.g. ex-ante suitability report as part of the Personal Investment Recommendation generated out of Wealth Navigator, PRIIP KID and UCITS KIID).

8. Elective professional clients

8.1. What conditions must retail clients meet to be re-categorized as elective professional?

Elective professional clients are retail clients whose opt-up request to be re-categorized as an elective professional got approved because the following conditions, as indicated below, are met:

Condition #1: Having made the opt-up request on their own initiative.

  • Qualitative test - Condition #2: (Individual clients) Having sufficient knowledge and experience to understand investment risks and make investment decisions. (Legal entity clients) The individual authorized representatives who can be order givers having sufficient knowledge and experience to understand investment risks and make investment decisions on behalf of the legal entity client;
  • Quantitative test - At least two of the following conditions must be met:
  • Condition #3: Having carried out transactions, in significant size (at least EUR 10,000), on the relevant market at an average frequency of 10 per quarter over the previous four quarters;
  • Condition #4: Having a portfolio (including cash deposits and financial instruments) exceeding €500,000; and
  • Condition #5: (Individual clients) Working or having worked in the financial sector for at least 1 year in a professional position, which requires the knowledge of the transactions or services envisaged. (Legal entity clients) The individual authorized representatives who can be order givers working or having worked in the financial sector for at least 1 year in a professional position, which requires the knowledge of the transactions or services envisaged.

For the qualitative test to be passed, conditions #1 and #2 must be met - it is a sine qua non requirement, i.e. if conditions #1 and #2 are not met, then, even if at least two of conditions #3, #4 and #5 are met, that retail client cannot become an elective professional.

For the quantitative test to be passed, at least two of conditions #3, #4 and #5 must be met.

8.2. Can a retail client be encouraged to make an opt-up request?

No. It is strictly forbidden: opt-up requests can only - strictly - be made by the client on own initiative.

This is because a retail client cannot be encouraged to waive the investor protection it is afforded by MiFID II.

BJBE is obliged to inform the client that they have the right to request a different category, and BJBE fulfils this obligation by giving the "Investment Services at Julius Baer - Basic Client Information" brochure to the client at onboarding.

However, BJBE (and the relationship manager (assistant) / investment advisor) can do no more than inform the client that they have this right: it is then the client the one who has to exercise it, i.e. only the client can make the opt-up request on own initiative.

8.3. If a client is “sophisticated” or “qualified”, does that mean that he/she should be categorized as elective professional?

No. Just because a retail client is sophisticated or qualified (e.g. the client holds an Finance degree) does not mean that they are automatically eligible to become an elective professional. A client is eligible to become an elective professional if they meet the required minimum conditions described under Section I, question 8.1.

8.4. What knowledge and experience are elective professional clients presumed to have?

Elective professional clients are presumed to have good knowledge and experience to understand investment risks and make investment decisions (however, less than per se professionals or eligible counterparties).

8.5. What product restrictions do elective professional clients face?

Unlike retail clients, elective professionals can invest in high-risk products like CoCo bonds. They also face less restrictions than retail clients in terms of target market and BJBE internal policy framework rules.

8.6. What information must elective professional clients receive?

Both elective professional and per se professional clients receive a more limited amount of information than retail clients pre-trade. For instance, while elective professional and per se professional clients still need to be provided with the UCITS KIID when investing in a UCITS and the PRIIP KID when investing in a PRIIP, they do not need to be provided with the ex-ante suitability report part of the Personal Investment Recommendation generated out of Wealth Navigator when BJBE renders investment advice to them.

8.7. What if an elective professional client no longer meets the minimum conditions required to be categorized as such?

Clients are responsible for providing BJBE with accurate information – BJBE relies on the accuracy of the information they provide and which is used to categorize them, and they agree to inform BJBE of any change in that information that may alter their categorization.

However, the relationship manager (assistant) / investment advisor must immediately let Business Risk Management ("BRM") know should they become aware that a client no longer fulfils the required minimum conditions described under Section I, question 8.1, and then BRM will assess and take appropriate action accordingly.

8.8. What if the individual authorized representatives of a legal entity client that has been re-categorized as elective professional (either for all asset classes or only one) change?

In this case, the relationship manager (assistant) / investment advisor must immediately inform BRM so they can jointly assess whether condition #5, if the re-categorization was dependent on it being met, continues to be met. Such assessment must be documented by BRM. If it is, then the legal entity client remains categorized as elective professional. If it is not, it must be re-categorized back to retail.

9. Per se professional clients 

9.1. What conditions must clients meet to be categorized as per se professional? 

Per se professional clients are either:

  • Entities required to be authorized or regulated to operate in financial markets (e.g. credit institutions, investment firms, insurance companies, collective investment schemes and their management companies, etc.); or

  • Large undertakings meeting at least 2 of the following 3 conditions:

    • Balance sheet total: EUR 20,000,000;
    • Net turnover: EUR 40,000,000; and
    • Own funds: EUR 2,000,000; or

  • National and regional governments, central banks, international and supranational institutions (e.g. the World Bank, the IMF, the ECB, the EIB, etc.); or

  • Other institutional investors whose main activity is to invest in financial instruments (including entities dedicated to the securitization of assets or other financing transactions).

Per se professional clients:

  • Are only legal entities.
  • Are presumed to have a very good knowledge and experience to understand investment risks and make investment decisions.
  • Like elective professional clients, can invest in high-risk products like CoCo bonds, face less restrictions than retail clients in terms of target market, legal and BJBE internal policy framework rules, and receive a more limited amount of information than retail clients pre-trade. For instance, while they still need to be provided with the UCITS KIID when investing in a UCITS, they do not need to be provided with the ex-ante suitability report part of the Personal Investment Recommendation generated out of Wealth Navigator when BJBE renders investment advice to them.

9.2. How can clients be categorized as per se professional? 

Clients can be categorized as per se professional:

  • Proactively by BJBE if the relationship manager (assistant) / investment advisor detects that the client fulfils the required minimum conditions described under Section I, question 9.1, or
  • Because (an authorized representative of) the client requests so and it is confirmed that the client fulfils the required minimum conditions described under Section I, question 9.1.

For the re-categorization process and procedure, please refer to Section II, sub-section 3.

9.3. What if a per se professional client no longer meets the minimum conditions required to be categorized as such? 

Clients are responsible for providing BJBE with accurate information – BJBE relies on the accuracy of the information they provide and which is used to categorize them, and they agree to inform BJBE of any change in that information that may alter their categorization.

However, the relationship manager (assistant) / investment advisor must immediately let BRM know should they become aware that a client no longer fulfils the required minimum conditions described under , and then BRM will assess and take appropriate action accordingly.

In addition, Compliance performs an annual review of per se professional clients to ensure that they still meet the minimum required conditions, and they will contact the respective relationship manager (assistant) / investment advisor if they no longer do.

10. Eligible counterparty clients 

10.1. What conditions must clients meet to be categorized as eligible counterparty? 

Eligible counterparty clients are per se professional clients (i.e. clients that fulfil the required minimum conditions described under Section I, question 9.1) that took the last possible step in waiving investor protection by requesting to be treated as an eligible counterparty.

10.2. What are the key differences between these eligible counterparties and the rest of categories?

Eligible counterparty clients:

  • Like per se professional clients, are only legal entities and are presumed to have a very good knowledge and experience to understand investment risks and make investment decisions.
  • Like elective and per se professional clients, can invest in high-risk products like CoCo bonds, and face less restrictions than retail clients in terms of target market, legal and internal policy framework rules.
  • Unlike the rest of categories, are not covered by the best execution policy and receive a very limited amount of information pre-trade. For instance, they do not need to be provided with the UCITS KIID, nor the PRIIP KID, nor the ex-ante suitability report part of the Personal Investment Recommendation generated out of Wealth Navigator when BJBE renders investment advice to them. 

10.3. How can clients be categorized as eligible counterparty? 

Clients can be categorized as eligible counterparty because (an authorized representative of) the client requests so and it is confirmed that the client fulfils the required minimum conditions described under Section I, question 10.1.

For the re-categorization process and procedure, please refer to Section II, sub-section 4.

10.4. What if an eligible counterparty client no longer meets the minimum conditions required to be categorized as such?

Clients are responsible for providing BJBE with accurate information – BJBE relies on the accuracy of the information they provide and which is used to categorize them, and they agree to inform BJBE of any change in that information that may alter their categorization.

However, the relationship manager (assistant) / investment advisor must immediately let BRM know should they become aware that a client no longer fulfils the required minimum conditions described under Section I, question 10.1, and then BRM will assess and take appropriate action accordingly.

In addition, Compliance performs an annual review of per se professional clients to ensure that they still meet the minimum required conditions, and they will contact the respective relationship manager (assistant) / investment advisor if they no longer do.

Section II – Client re-categorization

1. Opting up (to elective professional)

1.1. What does opting up mean?

Opting up is the process where a retail client asks to be treated as an elective professional, enabling the client to waive some of the protection that MiFID II had granted them as a retail investor.

In order to ensure that the client is fully aware of this, the relationship manager (assistant) / investment advisor provides and explains the "Notice Regarding Categorisation as Retail, Professional (Elective or Per Se) or Eligible Counterparty Client" to the client as soon as possible after the client requests to opt up.

1.2. How is the opt-up process (from retail client to elective professional) and what are the required documentation and forms?

  • Step 1. The client (the account holder in case of an individual account or the authorized representative in the case of a legal entity client) expresses his/her wish to opt up on their own initiative.
  • Step 2. The relationship manager (assistant) / investment advisor informs the client (or authorized representative) of the implications that an approved opt-up request will have by sending and explaining to them the "Notice Regarding Categorisation as Retail, Professional (Elective or Per Se) or Eligible Counterparty Client" and making sure the client is aware that he/she/it will be afforded less investor protection.
  • Step 3. The client (the account holder in case of an individual account or the authorized representative in the case of a legal entity client) makes an formal opt-up request by filling out and sending the relationship manager (assistant) / investment advisor the corresponding "Application". The relationship manager (assistant) / investment advisor creates a TAP contact note documenting it so that, if the communication channel was:
    • Email, he/she attaches the email exchange with the client (or authorized representative) to the TAP contact note.
    • Telephone, he/she indicates the details of the call in the TAP contact note: (i) who called who (e.g. the relationship manager called account participant XYZ), (ii) the date and time of the call and (iii) from BJBE's perspective, whether it was a landline or a cell phone line conversation;
    • A face-to-face meeting, he/she indicates the details of the face-to-face meeting.
  • Step 4. The relationship manager (assistant) / investment advisor assesses whether the conditions declared as met by the client are really met, by completing the corresponding re-categorization assessment XLS (see below). The procedure under Section II, question 1.3 is used. If the declared conditions are really met, the relationship manager (assistant) / investment advisor gathers the required supporting documentation, fills out the "Internal Request", and forwards the whole package (re-categorization assessment XLS, supporting documentation and "Internal Request") to BRM.

2. Partially opting up (to elective professional for alternative investment funds / subordinated capital products)

2.1 Can a retail client partially opt up to elective professional?

Yes. A client may opt up partially to elective professional either for subordinated capital products (like CoCo bonds) or alternative investment funds.

In such case, the client will be treated as elective professional for either of those asset classes while as retail for the rest of the asset classes.

However, even if the client makes a partial opt-up request, he/she/it must still meet all the required minimum conditions described under Section I, question 8.1 for that specific asset class. Please refer to the procedure under Section II, question 2.3 for further details.

2.2. How is the partial opt-up process and what are the required documentation and forms?

It is like the regular opt-up process, as described under , but adapted to partial opt-up requests:

  • In step 3, a partial "Application" is used.
  • In step 4, a partial opt-up request type is selected in the re-categorization assessment XLS, and the "Internal Request" is filled out accordingly. The procedure under Section II, question 2.3 is used.

5. Opting down (to retail)

5.1. What does opting down mean?

Opting down means gaining MiFID II investor protection. If a professional (elective or per se) or eligible counterparty client opts down, they become retail and they therefore gain the protection that MiFID II grants to retail investors.

5.2. What is the opt-down process (from eligible counterparty, elective professional or per se professional to retail) and what are the required documentation and forms?

  • Step 1. The client (or authorized representative in the case of a legal entity client) expresses his/her wish to opt down.
  • Step 2. The relationship manager (assistant) / investment advisor creates a TAP contact note documenting it so that, if the communication channel was:
    • Email, he/she attaches the email exchange with the client (or authorized representative) to the TAP contact note.
    • Telephone, he/she indicates the details of the call in the TAP contact note: (i) who called who (e.g. the relationship manager called account participant XYZ), (ii) the date and time of the call and (iii) from BJBE's perspective, whether it was a landline or a cell phone line conversation;
    • A face-to-face meeting, he/she indicates the details of the face-to-face meeting.
  • Step 3. The relationship manager (assistant) / investment advisor communicates it to BRM, making reference to the TAP contact note created.
  • Step 4. BRM requests Front Support to:
    • Customize and send to the client the dedicated re-categorization letter together with the "Notice Regarding Categorisation as Retail, Professional (Elective or Per Se) or Eligible Counterparty Client", and
    • Create a TAP contact note documenting and archiving the latter.
  • Step 5. Front Support does so and uploads the 1) customized letter and the 2) confirmation email from BRM to the CDS tool under task "Other or complex cases" with Sub-Task "Opt-down professional to retail". CDS receives the request and uploads the related documentation to FIO and changes the related static data in TAP.

5.3. Can a professional or eligible counterparty client partially opt down to retail?

No. If a professional (elective or per se) or eligible counterparty client requests to opt down, they will do so but totally.

5.4. Is it possible to opt down to "one level below"?

No. If a professional (elective or per se) or eligible counterparty client requests to opt down, they will do so but to retail in all cases. It is therefore not possible, for example, for an eligible counterparty client to opt down to elective professional. However, once back as retail, the client will still have the right to request to be re-categorized differently.
