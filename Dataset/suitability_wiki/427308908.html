Background

There is no regulatory obligation to establish a target market beyond the requirements set out in MiFID regulations. However, based on risk considerations and to ensure that the right product is distributed to the right client, JB has introduced the JB Distributor Target Market concept.

→ The JB Distributor Target Market reflects the distribution strategy and risk appetite of the bank.

Scope

The JB DTM applies to all Advisory Service Models and for all solicitation types (incl. unsolicited transactions).

Unsolicited transactions are subject to the same checks, with the aim of preventing clients under an Advisory Service Model from holding prohibited financial instruments in their portfolios

How is the Target Market defined?

The JB DTM is defined on instrument level (e.g. non-traditional funds, private equity, digital assets) based on the risk, complexity and investment horizon of the product type. 

For example, a private equity investment is not suited for retail investors or clients with a low risk tolerance due to its inherent product risks, and is intended solely for investors with a long-term investment horizon. Therefore the investment is blocked for the following investors.

  • Retail Client
  • Financial Risk Ability Low
  • Investment Strategy Fixed Income and Income
  • Investment Horizon >1 year

How does the check work?

The JB DTM checks the distribution scope defined for a financial instrument type (e.g. Digital Assets) against the client attributes (Client Classification, Financial Risk Ability, Time Horizon and Investment Strategy). If the client attributes do not match with the distribution scope, a JB DTM alert is triggered, informing the RM that the product cannot be advised nor sold (even upon an unsolicited request) to a specific client.

→ The JB DTM alert is a hard block and cannot be overridden by the RM, based on the RM's own judgement, unless the alert is triggered based on wrong data (->Hard Block Override Procedure)

 The JB DTM is part of the Product & Services Test and applies in addition to the 'classic' suitability check. The JB DTM compares product attributes with objective client data, whereas the 'classic' suitability test also considers the portfolio composition.

What happens if the DTM changes?

Changes to the JB DTM — whether initiated by Julius Baer (JB) or resulting from product classifications received from data providers such as SIX may result in the blocking of additional purchases (top-ups) of a financial instrument already held in a client’s portfolio. The block also applies for unsolicited orders. Existing positions booked in the client portfolio are not effected. 

What happens if a location does not have a client classification in place?

If no client classification is in place, client classification is not checked and no alert is triggered.

What happens in case of old Service Models without Investment Strategy?

If no Investment Strategy is defined in the Service Model, the Investment Strategy is not checked and no alert is triggered.

FCR (Fondo de Capital a Riesgo)

FCR's are Private Equity Funds in which Retail clients are able to invest in, if they are registered with the Spanish regulator as "is Retail Eligible". In order to allow such investments for Retail clients the JB DTM hard block for Retail Clients has been removed for certain valors. The DTM blocks for "Financial Risk Ability" and "Time Horizon" remain the same.
