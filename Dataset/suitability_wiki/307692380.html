2. Description of the alert

Product Risk Rating (PRR)

Every financial instrument within the JB investment universe has a Product Risk Rating. It allows us to compare the financial risk of different kind of investment products and asset classes. There are three relevant risk factors – market risk, credit risk, and the liquidity risk penalty – which are all incorporated into the PRR. The PRR is a value between 0-7 (the riskier the instrument, the higher the value).

Note that PRR is a risk measurement for single instruments – it does not consider portfolio aspects. Additionally, the PRR can change during the lifetime of the instrument (as the inputs used to calculate it change as the market changes).


Consolidated Product Risk (CPR)

The Consolidated Product Risk is the weighted average of the PRR of each position within an investment portfolio. In investment portfolios, the CPR considers positive and negative cash balances as well as long and short positions. 

For the purposes of calculating the CPR, the following applies:

  • Any amount of credit (e.g. a Lombard Loan) is deducted from the size of the portfolio (but it is not assigned a PRR, therefore not included in the CPR calculation). Thus it is possible to have a higher CPR than the average of all positions' PRR.
  • Cash is assigned a PRR of zero as it is considered as a risk free cushion
  • The leverage of Lombard Loans: If a client has a Lombard Loan, the alert automatically considers the respective leverage. By default, 100% of the amount of all Lombard Loans booked to the advisory portfolio is considered as reinvested and hence considered by the CPR. If the loan is not or only partially invested the leverage can be amended in the Portfolio Configuration (→How to amend the Lombard Loan Configuration)

Note that the CPR does not address FX risk, impacts of hedging & diversification, or instrument correlation.


The below described Risk Budget and the alert triggering of the CPR and the CPR incl. leverage is identical. Details to the CPR with leverage calculation and the considerations when recommending leverage can be found in section "6. Further information on CPR incl. leverage" of this chapter.

The available CPR risk budget is defined by the contractual investment strategy of the mandate. This is illustrated in the below table:

3. What triggers this alert?

A CPR alert is triggered when the CPR is beyond the risk budget of the contractual investment strategy. This may happen either:

a) in the Advisory Session as a result of a newly proposed transaction, or

b) in the Client Book as a result of overnight portfolio risk monitoring, e.g. due to price fluctuations or because a Lombard Loan has been booked into the portfolio etc. and

    • there is a breach of the official threshold for 5 consecutive days (Time) or
    • immediately if the breach is significant: CPR results in excess of risk budget by 0.5 or more (Severity)

=> This means, that the overnight alert in the portfolio monitoring is designed to tolerate minor exceedances as long as these are not persistent.

Validity of Resolution Notes (RN)

The Resolution Note is valid until 

  • the snooze period ends or
  • the situation “worsens” or “improves” during the lifetime of the RN (→Worsening/Improvement Concept - Wiki)
  • if a new transaction leads to an increase in CPR

If the alert is retriggered during the snooze period, the client must be contacted and informed again about the new situation. Reference to the first contact is not sufficient.

I received a CPR alert, due to the fact that the put has a PRR of 7, but with the long put structure, the actual risk is reduced, hence I apply “Methodology exception” and snooze the alert for 3 month until expiry.

6. Further information on CPR incl. leverage

If borrowed money using Lombard Loans is invested into a portfolio, the risk/return profile of the investment portfolio is changed due to the “leverage” factor. Based on the degree of the leverage (borrowed money vs. equity capital), Wealth Navigator adds a surcharge on the Consolidated Product Risk (CPR) in order to consider leverage when assessing the investment suitability towards the investment strategy of the client.


6.1 General questions

A. What needs to be considered when using leverage?

Regulators across several jurisdictions but also good practice in terms of advisory services require the front facing staff to provide proper risk disclosure when a client wants to invest using a Lombard Loan and assess if the portfolio risk is still in line with the investment strategy of the client when using leverage.

B. What to do if a client wants to invest in leverage?

  • Based on the Client Investment Profile (CIP) assess if the client fulfils the required overall risk tolerance and overall financial risk ability to invest with a leverage. The supporting matrix (pls. see below “Matrix to estimate CPR incl. leverage based on different leverage ratios”) helps to estimate based on the current CPR of the client portfolio what degree of leverage ratio would be suitable.
  • Explain to the client the effect of leverage on the risk/return profile and ensure that the increased risks are understood. For this purpose, use the provided Lombard Risk Disclosure appendix which is attached to the credit agreement. Discuss with the client if the CIP needs to be adapted assessing carefully if client can bear higher risk (and thus if client’s overall financial risk ability and overall risk tolerance in CIP can be increased). Advise on the Lombard Loan usage and parameters such as currency, appropriate maturity, and amount of Lombard Loan in due consideration of the assessed client suitability and general credit limits provided by credit risk (TEMOS).
  • Ask the client to sign all required documents such as a credit agreement which includes the risk disclosure appendix to draw Lombard Loan(s).

Please follow these steps also if a client wants to substantially increase an existing leverage utilization.


6.2 Calculation

A. How is the CPR incl. leverage-alert derived?

The basis factors to assess the investment suitability including the consideration of “leverage” is the CPR of the portfolio and the leverage ratio. The higher the leverage ratio is, the higher is the risk surcharge to the CPR as the risk/return profile is also increased based on leverage ratio.

Calculation of portfolio leverage ratio:

Sum Lombard Loans in ref ccy / (AuM of portfolio in ref ccy + Sum Lombard Loans in ref ccy)

Example starting position:

  • Current CPR: 3.5
  • Sum Lombard Loan(s) fully reinvested: 2mio
  • AuM of portfolio (equity capital): 8mio


Leverage ratio = 2mio / (8mio + 2mio) = 0.2 -> 20%

CPR surcharge of 0.5 with leverage ratio of 20% (this surcharge is determined by an internal mapping table)

CPR incl. leverage = 3.5 + 0.5 = 4


Client wants to buy for 1.4mio fund XY by taking a Lombard Loan:

Leverage ratio = (2mio + 1.4mio) / (8mio + 2mio + 1.4mio) = 0.298 -> 29.8%

CPR surcharge of 0.8 with leverage ratio of 29.8% (this surcharge is determined by an internal mapping table)

CPR incl. leverage = 3.5 + 0.8 = 4.3

B. When is Wealth Navigator not able to assess the portfolio leverage?

If the Lombard Loan is booked in another portfolio than the portfolio where the borrowed amount is reinvested, then Wealth Navigator won’t be able to assess portfolio leverage due to missing data (money flows can’t be controlled, only booked loans).

For example, client has the following portfolios:

  • Advisory Portfolio 1 (incl. booked loan of 1mUSD, 500k reinvested) -> Wealth Navigator can calculate portfolio leverage
  • Advisory Portfolio 2 (without any loans, but 300k reinvested of loan from portfolio 1) -> Wealth Navigator can’t calculate portfolio leverage
  • Discretionary Mandate Portfolio 3 (loans can’t be booked into DM portfolios, but 200k is reinvested of loan from portfolio 1) -> Wealth Navigator is not used by Portfolio Management, therefore no portfolio leverage assessment per se.


6.3. Process

A. When does a CPR incl. leverage-alert occur and how can it be resolved?

The CPR incl. leverage alert handling is explained above.

 

B. How can the reinvested amount in Wealth Navigator portfolio configuration be changed?

By default, 100% of the amount of all Lombard Loans booked to the advisory portfolio is considered as reinvested. However, if the borrowed money is not or only partially invested in this advisory portfolio, i.e. borrowed money was transferred out of the portfolio or reinvested in another portfolio, then this can be specified in the portfolio configuration (under “Loan Configuration”).

With the reduction of the reinvested loan amount, the portfolio leverage ratio will be reduced in the consequence. Therefore, it is important that the circumstances are properly documented. Wealth Navigator supports you with automated contact note which is triggered when you adjust the Loan Configuration. The contact note shall refer to the conversation with the client (acknowledgement of the client about the setup and consequences e.g. that the leverage cannot be taken into the calculation of the CPR), and where the usage of the loan was discussed. It is also important that the reinvested amount is adapted accordingly if circumstances change.

Please maintain the reinvested amount in the portfolio configuration before you start a Wealth Navigator session to avoid an unnecessary alert.

As a general guidance, please book the Lombard loan into the portfolio where the borrowed money is reinvested. If this is not possible, because of the client instruction, the client shall be aware that no calculation can be done.


C. If there is a Discretionary Mandate, the loan cannot be booked on the same portfolio: What is the workaround?

See previous question and JBG-2064-00 Policy on Investment Process and Portfolio Management (chapter 6.1.2).  →JBG-2064-00 Policy on Investment Process and Portfolio Management v.4 (navexone.eu)

 

D. The final CPR risk depends also on where the loan is being invested: How is this applied?

For the CPR Assessment (pls. see 2.1 “Matrix to estimate CPR incl. leverage based on different leverage ratios”) the starting point is the current CPR of the client, and the leverage ratio. With these two parameters the estimated CPR can be checked. Please bear in mind, that the assumption for the assessment table is, that the client continues to invest in the same way. If the client invests the loan into more aggressive investments, the CPR will be higher and if the client invests more conservatively the CPR will likely not raise as much as assessed. →Handling of leveraged portfolios (using lombard loans)


E. Is there a system supported simulation option?

No, the pre-assessment is based on the matrix (pls. see 2.1 “Matrix to estimate CPR incl. leverage based on different leverage ratios”)


F. If the loan purpose is option 3 - e.g. 100% non-investment purposes – does this have to be adjusted manually as per loan configuration screen or can you set up the loan that this is a non-investment loan?

Yes, you need to adjust the loan configuration accordingly. Please note that the leverage ratio is not 0% even with the complete outscoring of the loan for reinvestment. There is still a risk factor for having a loan pledged with assets of the portfolio. 


G. How to find out if an existing loan was used for reinvestment?

If a CPR alert is triggered, this is the starting point for the client discussion and the client can be informed, that the bank has enhanced it's calculation methodology to systematically include the risk from leveraging a portfolio. Another touchpoint to discuss the purpose of the loan can be the rollover of a loan. In this case, consider amending the portfolio configuration even if no alert is being triggered.


H. What do we need to communicate to the client?

The client can fully benefit from the adjusted risk calculation in the advisory business if the loan is booked in the same portfolio where the re-investment takes place. When the loan is booked outside, the client shall be made aware, that the bank can not systematically ensure, that the risk is still within the client's risk appetite. This limitation is also written in the risk disclosure document ("multiple portfolios of a borrower"). 


I. Does the alert block the booking of a loan until clarified?

No, the loan process is not affected by any alert as it is only triggered after the loan is booked. It is therefore important to pre-assess if the client has still sufficient risk budget to use lending for re-investment purpose. 


J. If the client has a loan which will be renewed on a monthly basis: Is there a need of doing a new Wealth Navigator session?

With regards to the validity period it is not required that the portfolio configuration and the loan have the same tenor. The portfolio configuration can for example be set to 6 month although the loan is rolled over monthly. Please ensure, that you adapt the configuration if the client situation changes, even if this is before the loan configuration expires.


K. Are Sharia compliant loans, Commodity Murabaha Financing also considered in the CPR calculation?

Yes, they are considered in the CPR calculation as loans. 


L. How is the process for advisory offices to book the new loan (as a suitability assessment is required)?

The process to set up a loan does not change, neither is the proof of a suitability assessment required. The suitability assessment is important to understand beforehand if there is still sufficient risk budget for a client in line with his/her investment strategy. 


6.4 Methodology

A. When the client has a fixed income strategy but only invests in high yield bonds then the CPR is already breached. The CPR with leverage does enhance this issue and does not solve an existing problem.

The inclusion of the leverage factor does not change the existing models or strategy definitions, incl. SAA ranges. 

 

B. If the strategy is changed due to the client’s investment preferences this might trigger a SAA violation?

Please see the previous question. This is an existing issue that can occur for CPR calculation with or without having a leveraged portfolio. 


C. If the client has segregated accounts (e.g. UK RnD) and the loan is booked on another portfolio within the same account than the reinvestment. However, the segregated portfolios are considered as one by the client and have the same mandate & strategy. Is there a way to apply the CPR with leverage on consolidated level?

There is the possibility to consolidate portfolios under the conditions: same ZR, mandate and strategy

Impact and handling: alerts (all, not just CPR) are triggered on the consolidated portfolio only (i.e. as one single mandate). The client must be aware of the consolidation. Make sure to process the advisory session on the consolidated portfolio shown in Wealth Navigator but bear in mind to select the real existing portfolio when placing an order. Any additional local guidance must be adhered to (e.g. advisory contract mentioning both portfolios).
