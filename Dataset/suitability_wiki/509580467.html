Best practices

Strategic Asset Allocation Exposure

  • Consider the alert at portfolio level, not at account level.

  • Archive evidence in the respective TAP contact note of:

    • Having notified the client of the existence of the alert within 5 business days (one way communication is fine).

    • If you snooze the alert and create a Resolution Note because your client does not want to make changes to his asset allocation, adapt the mandate strategy or complete a new CIP with a new investment strategy, of (1) him confirming this and (2) of you informing him that you will only be able to provide him with advice that brings his asset allocation closer or back to the allowed threshold.

→ Remember the Resolution Note is only valid:

          • Until the snoozed period ends;
          • The situation “worsens” or “improves” during the lifetime of the Resolution Note; or
          • A new trade increases the deviation. 

→ If you have to snooze the alert again and create a new Resolution Note, do not just refer to the first one, but follow the guidance given above again.

    • If you and your client adapt the mandate strategy or complete a new CIP with a new investment strategy, of him agreeing to this.

    • If the client having agreed to an adaptation of mandate strategy or completion of a new CIP with a different investment strategy.


Consolidated Product Risk

  • Consider the alert at portfolio level, not at account level.

  • If you use “methodology exception”, clearly explain why the formula / way to calculate CPR is wrong (e.g. CPR exists because factors such as hedging were not considered in the calculation done by Wealth Navigator).

  • Archive evidence in the respective TAP contact note of:
    • Having notified the client of the existence of the alert within 5 business days (one way communication is fine).
    • If you snooze the alert and create a Resolution Note because your client does not want to reduce the portfolio CPR below the maximum risk budget, adapt the mandate strategy or complete a new CIP with a new investment strategy, of (1) him confirming this and (2) of you informing him that you will only be able to provide him with advice that brings the CPR closer or back to the allowed threshold.

→ Remember the Resolution Note is only valid:

          • Until the snoozed period ends;
          • The situation “worsens” or “improves” during the lifetime of the Resolution Note; or
          • A new trade increases the deviation.

→ If you have to snooze the alert again and create a new Resolution Note, do not just refer to the first one, but follow the guidance given above again.

    • If you and your client adapt the mandate strategy or complete a new CIP with a new investment strategy, of him agreeing to this.

Overall Investment Strategy

  • IMPORTANT. Remember that consistency between the CIP investment strategy and the weighted average of all mandate strategies (i.e. the Overall Investment Strategies) is required in order to ensure suitability in all portfolios with an advisory and discretionary mandate. Neither investment advice nor portfolio management can be provided without suitability having been ensured.

      Because of this, whenever an Overall Investment Strategy ("OIS") alert has been triggered, you must:

    • Adapt the portfolio mandate strategy(ies), or
    • Have the client complete a new CIP with a new CIP investment strategy if the client’s circumstances have changed (e.g. risk ability/tolerance, etc.), or
    • Re-configure the weight of the portfolios in the account.

  • HANDLING AN OIS ALERT. An OIS alert can be justified only if the conditions outlined below are fulfilled: 

I.    The client has agreed to have a consolidated monitoring over several mandates as described here. If the client's account only has one portfolio, then this condition can be disregarded.
II.   You do the Consolidated Product Risk (“CPR”) and Strategic Asset Allocation Exposure (“SAAE”) checks at account level, and you confirm that neither alert would be triggered (to do so, please use this guidance). If the client's account only has one portfolio, then neither of these alerts must be triggered at portfolio level.
III.  (*) You inform the client about the existence of the OIS alert, and advise him against keeping it.
IV.  (*) The client still wishes to keep it. In such case, you document in a TAP contact note:

A. Having given a risk warning to the client, and

B. That it is the client’s explicit decision to keep it (important: never proactively encouraged by you).

* You may take care of fulfilling conditions III and IV by customizing the following template and sending it to the client.

Client template

Dear [client name],

We would like to bring to your attention that, when considering all your investment mandates collectively, the overall risk level currently stands slightly above the target defined in your Client Investment Profile (“CIP”).

While this positioning may offer potential for enhanced returns, it also implies a higher degree of volatility and downside risk than originally intended. As part of our commitment to responsible stewardship of your assets, we generally advise maintaining alignment between your actual investment strategy and your approved CIP.

With this in mind, we kindly invite you to confirm whether:

You are comfortable continuing with the current level of risk, acknowledging that this entails greater market sensitivity and potential fluctuations in capital value;

or

You would prefer to review and adjust certain mandates—or consider updating your CIP—to restore full consistency with your established risk parameters.

We strongly encourage taking the opportunity to reassess this position in light of your broader financial goals and risk appetite. Our team is ready to support you in evaluating the most suitable path forward.

Please do not hesitate to reach out—we would be pleased to discuss this further at your convenience.

Best regards,

[Your name]

V. Finally, you:

      • Justify the OIS alert with “Client accepts risk”,
      • Confirm in the “comment” section that there is no CPR alert nor SAAE alert account level, and add the risk warning including the client’s decision (store any written evidence in the TAP contact note), and
      • Snooze the OIS alert for “1 month”.

N.B.! Remember that the justification will be considered valid only for 1 month.

  • EXAMPLES. Justifying an OIS alert as long as the above conditions are met could be necessary, for example (list non-exhaustive):

    • In the case of newly funded portfolios.
    • If the client’s CIP investment strategy is “Growth” but the client has chosen “Capital Gain” to be most flexible in his mandate: as long as the above conditions are fulfilled, it is possible to fulfil his need.
    • If the client has portfolios with other banks and see their investment strategy holistically and uses BJBE for his private equity investments, for example: in such case, it would make sense to have a “Balanced” CIP strategy if they answer all questions honestly, but still have a “Capital Gain” mandate to have most flexibility, even if that results in an OIS, which could be justified as long as the above conditions are fulfilled.

Remember! After that 1 month, if WN triggers another OIS alert, please repeat the process outlined above (except for conditions I, III and IV.A – i.e. contacting the client). To document that it was the client's decision to keep the OIS alert (condition IV.B), simply make reference to the TAP contact note corresponding to the first time you justified the OIS alert as per this process and where you documented that this condition (IV.B) was fulfilled.
