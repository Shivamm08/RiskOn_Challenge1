2. Description of the alert

A “Concentration Risk on Single Positions” is defined as a position in a specific instrument in the portfolio with a weight that exceeds the threshold defined for that asset class. Therefore, the price movements of the instrument can have an outsized impact on the overall portfolio performance.

The below table summarizes the thresholds applicable for each asset class:

Asset classThreshold
Equity

10%

Structured Product

10%

(Single) Bond

10%

Non-traditional Fund

10%

Fund

10%

Fund (UCITS, CH-registered)

20%


3. What triggers this alert?

The Concentration Risk on Single Positions can be triggered either:

a) in the Advisory Session as a result of a newly proposed transaction that exceeds the max. defined threshold for this asset class, or

b) in the Client Book as a result of overnight portfolio risk monitoring as the weight of an existing position has increased beyond the asset class-specific threshold (typically this is the result of positive price performance in the instrument, but it could also happen for other reasons, such as an outflow from the portfolio). The alert is triggered when

    • there is a breach of the asset class specific threshold for 5 consecutive days or
    • immediately if the breach is significant: the official threshold is exceeded by more than 10% 

=> This means that the alert is calibrated to allow for small excesses, as long as the excess is not persistent.


4. How can this alert be resolved?

Resolving the alert depends on the type of the alert:


A. Advisory Session (session alert)

Solicitation Type

Duty

Documentation (Justification / Contact Note)

Actively solicited

RM/IA must reduce the investment amount in the transaction until the alert is not triggered anymore to make the investment advice suitable

or

RM/IA must not proceed with the transaction as long as the alert is shown, because the investment advice is unsuitable


Reverse solicited

RM/IA must advise against the transaction before its execution if it results in a breach

or

RM/IA must advice to reduce investment amount in the transaction or other positions of the same asset class

If client accepts the risk and wants to proceed -> RM/IA to document the client’s decision and rationale in the alert justification/ contact note as it is described here: →Justification rationale (dropdown values) for "client accepts risk"

Unsolicited

RM/IA must warn the order giver of the breach before executing the transaction

If client accepts the risk and wants to proceed -> RM/IA to document the warning in the alert justification/ contact note as described here: →Justification rationale (dropdown values) for "client accepts risk"

Important: In the event of any data issues in the system or in dedicated permitted use cases (e.g. workarounds) it is possible to overrule the alert and document it according to the defined process →Hard Block Override Procedure


B. Client Book (overnight portfolio monitoring alert)

DutyResolving the alert & Documentation (Resolution / Contact Note)

RM/IA must inform the client within 5 business days



RM/IA to create an investment proposal to address the reduction of the position's weight below the asset class specific threshold and provide it to the client. Use resolution note “investment proposal sent” (alert will be snoozed for 30 days)

RM/IA to consider moving strategic holdings to a non-trading account

RM/IA to create a resolution note in the client book if the client does not want to make changes and accepts the risk (“client accepts risk” – also see examples below).

Please note: In case of a single resolution note (use the check Action symbol [  ] in the line of the respective alert), the justification rationale are available to support documentation: (→Justification rationale (dropdown values) for "client accepts risk"). If the client could not be reached, RM/IA to select “client not reached” in the alert justification and document 1) the date when the client was approached and 2) the channel in the contact note.


Validity of Resolution Notes (RN)

The Resolution Note is valid until 

  • the snooze period ends or
  • the situation “worsens” or “improves” during the lifetime of the RN (→Worsening/Improvement Concept)
  • if a new transaction leads to an increase in concentration

If the alert is retriggered during the snooze period, the client must be contacted and informed again about the new situation. Reference to the first contact is not sufficient.


5. Justification examples

Use case

Examples

 Session alert

Account holder Mr. X called me to place some unsolicited buy orders. I informed him that exposure to the JB Next Generation fund is 16% of the portfolio, which has triggered a concentration risk. I advised him to reduce the position to 10%.

He would like to hold the position, as it is a long-term conviction holding. He agreed to review the situation again in six months.

Changes in mandate configuration

I met with the Authorized Signatory Mrs X on April 16th for a portfolio review. I told her that the portfolio is very concentrated in Zoom Communications (22%) and Nvidia (25%), due last year’s strong performance. I advised to sell Nvidia, as it’s rated Reduce by JB. She asked for my opinion on Zoom, and I informed that I cannot give advice as the stock is not covered.

She agreed to sell Nvidia and reduce Zoom to 15%. As she does not want to be notified of Zoom again the alert was snoozed for 12 months.


6. Q&A relating to changes in 2025

A. Why do we need to implement the single position threshold changes?

The concentration risk changes, while triggered by regulatory requirements (from Swiss regulator FINMA), additionally consider our contractual obligation to the client as part of the advisory agreement. The concentration risk alert intends not only to mitigate counterparty/market risk, but also serves to ensure that the advice provided by the Bank is in line with the client’s strategy i.e. aiming at a certain return as well as sufficient diversification and not being excessively exposed to single products (which includes cash-like products). In scope of the respective check are all financial instruments (which excludes accounts or time deposits, although they might have similar risk elements as e.g. notes). This reflects the legal interpretation of the circular, views of the relevant framework owners / 2nd line functions and the implementation logic endorsed by the Investment Suitability Board (ISB)


B. Who is affected by the change?

This change affects the following BCs with all respective RMLs: CH/GG, LUX, MC.

 

C. What are the changes to single position concentration thresholds?

The single position concentration thresholds for single bonds, non-traditional funds, and other funds that are not UCITS or not registered in Switzerland will be lowered to 10% - all other instrument types where already before at 10%. In addition, we also changing the underlying FICATs that are subject to the check.


D. Are there any exceptions to the new 10% threshold?

Yes, UCITS and Swiss-registered funds will remain at 20%.


E. How will the new concentration thresholds affect existing holdings?

Existing holdings above 10% will trigger overnight alerts in Advice Premium & Exclusive/Advanced mandates, unless they already have a valid justification (i.e. snoozed alert). What is not permitted is the active solicitation of such products above the threshold; that does not mean that the client cannot hold these products if they explicitly wish to and are comfortable with the exposure (the alert can still be justified on this basis).


F. Can previously applied justifications be used for new alerts?

Yes, previously applied justifications (e.g., “client accepts risk”) remain effective and will not retrigger alerts. These alerts will stay snoozed until the initially agreed end of the snooze period.


G. Is there any way to make it easier to deal with alerts?

Yes, the ONE-WAY notification (OWN) feature will make it easier to deal with alerts because it allows for a standardized alert notification document to be dispatched directly from Wealth Navigator to the client's chosen e-channel ,thus streamlining the process of informing clients about monitoring alerts. Specifically, after enabling OWN, the justification option "Client informed" becomes available in Wealth Navigator, which can be used to justify monitoring alerts means that for certain alerts, such as concentration risk on single positions and issuer concentration, a qualified contact with active client feedback will no longer be required. Please find more information on OWN, on the dedicated Wiki Page.


H. What is the timeframe for communicating alerts to clients?

Alerts must be communicated to clients within five business days, however there will be an exception from the KRI sampling until end of 2025 (see next question to see if the deadline can’t be met)


I. What happens to alerts that remain unresolved by the end of 2025 or longer?

Alerts that remain unresolved by the end of 2025 or longer will be reintroduced into the sampling and reflected in the scorecard results


J. What happens with legacy service models like PSR, ISM or IA mandates in the European BCs? 

The concentration on single position and issuer concentration pre-trade checks are now business-critical (no overnight alerts) for those mandates (only relevant for RML Chile and Uruguay)
