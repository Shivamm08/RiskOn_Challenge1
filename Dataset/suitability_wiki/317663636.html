1. My client holds multiple financial instruments in his portfolio. If I only advise him to BUY more of one of them, does that mean that I am advising him to HOLD the rest (and that, for each of the rest, I must give a reason in the Personal Investment Recommendation (PIR) of why I advise him to hold)? 

No. You must only use the new “advice to hold” enhancement that went live with the June 2021 DiAS release when you advise your client, due to a specific reason, to hold a specific financial instrument which he/she/it already has in his/her/its portfolio.


For example, let’s say your client held Astrazeneca shares back in early 2021 when, at the very last stage of Phase III of its COVID-19 vaccine clinical trial, the company got bad press due to some volunteers getting seriously ill after receiving their test shot. Its share price plummeted but you were convinced that this was just a minor setback and that it would bounce back up because the vaccine would end up being approved. If back then you had advised your client to hold his/her/its shares, and of course if this “advice to hold” option had been available back then, then in this case you would have had to use it.


For more information on this new enhacement, please refer to the June 2021 DiAS release notes.

2. What is an “unsolicited”, “reverse solicited” and “solicited” scenario exactly?

As explained in the training, trades may be executed in an “unsolicited”, “reverse solicited” and “solicited” scenario.


In accordance with the way DiAS is currently set up, we at BJBE define those three solicitation scenarios as follows:

  • In an “unsolicited” scenario, it is the client who initiates the trade by giving an order, with no advice being given;
  • In a “reverse solicited” scenario, it is the client who requests to be provided with advice on what to do (buy/sell/hold) with respect to (a) specific financial instrument(s); and
  • In a “solicited” scenario, it is the RM who initiates the provision of advice to the client on what to do (buy/sell/hold) with respect to (a) specific financial instrument(s).

Now, please note that:

  • The way article 42 of Directive 2014/65/EU defines “reverse solicitation” is actually the way we at BJBE define the “unsolicited” scenario; and
  • This inconsistency between MiFID II and BJBE will be resolved by an upcoming DiAS release, which will adapt BJBE’s understanding of the “reverse solicited” scenario to the way MiFID II defines it.

In the meantime, however, please continue understanding “reverse solicitation” as described above.

3. Who can I ask for help to determine the solicitation scenario in which my client’s trade has been executed?

We are aware that the examples of solicitation scenarios given in Q&A #11 of the May 2021 MiFID II refresher training Q&As and in June’s training are too simple, that is, that the ones you may find in reality are far more complex.

As mentioned in both trainings, it is to everyone’s benefit that trades be documented clearly. Of course, for you to be able to do this, however, you first need to determine the solicitation scenario in which the trade was carried out.

5. I have identified a conflict of interest. How should I report it?

As explained in the training, you are required, as soon as you identify a potential or actual conflict of interest, to immediately report it to your local Compliance (virginie.etienne@juliusbaer.com and manuel.juanjuan@juliusbaer.com in Luxembourg, victor.garciaromero@juliusbaer.com and javier.delsaz@juliusbaer.com in Spain and rachel.furlong@juliusbaer.com in Ireland).

Your local Compliance will record the identified and reported conflict of interest in the conflict of interest register, which BJBE must keep in accordance with article 23 of Directive 2014/65/EU and article 35 of Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/565. Based on the risk it exposes BJBE to, your local Compliance will design and implement mitigating measures.

It is therefore imperative that as soon as you identify a potential or actual conflict of interest, you immediately report it. Like that, you will be contributing to minimizing BJBE exposure to risk.
