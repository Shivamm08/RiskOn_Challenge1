Managing advisory relationships after Client death

https://wiki.juliusbaer.com/spaces/fkh/pages/728286160/Managing+advisory+relationships+after+Client+death

Scope: BC CH & GUE / all RML (except RML UK & Guernsey → For those RML please see the Guernsey process or Switzerland process)

This article outlines the procedures and responsibilities for RMs upon becoming aware of the death of a client. It covers the handling of investment actions related to advisory mandates. 

ℹ️ This guidance complements the procedures outlined on the Inheritance Matters intranet page and the official Inheritance process documentation. For any account-related matters concerning deceased clients, please contact Account Opening Support (**1166#1) or refer to the AOS wiki page.


🔴 Immediate actions upon notification of death

As soon as you learn of a client’s passing from a reliable source — such as relatives, legal representatives, asset managers, or public announcements — you must act promptly (see Inheritance process).

1. Record the date of death in CLM/CRM

    • Log the information immediately in CLM/CRM (no later than 14 days after the passing away becomes aware).

    • Enter:
      • The actual date of death, if known.
      • Or, a provisional fictitious date if the exact date is pending confirmation. (Update once verified.)
    • Perform this entry at Partner Level in the system.

2. Notify Client Documentation Services (CDS) – Account Modification and forward the notification along with:

    • Confirmation of death
    • Source of information (e.g., funeral notice, letter from heir, court document)

💡 What happens to existing Advisory Mandates?

Advisory (and also discretionary) mandates remain valid after death and do not expire automatically until the written termination by the legal successors or other authorised parties.

However:

    • Any action requiring client consent now requires approval from the heirs or appointed executor.
    • If no heir or executor  has been identified, portfolio re-allocations or reinvestment should not be made, except in urgent cases and solely in the best interest of the heirs (). 

💡 Can an existing Power of Attorney (PoA) revoke an Advisory Mandate after the client’s death?

❌ No. An existing Power of Attorney (PoA) cannot revoke an advisory mandate following the death of the account holder.

💡 Which rules apply if my client was a Co-Holder on a Joint- or Custody-Account and the other Holder(s) are still alive?

The required actions differ depending on the account type.

Account TypeProcedure
Joint Account (JAC)

No changes required
- Succession documentation is not required.
- Remaining co-holder(s) retain full authority.
- They may initiate changes independently.
- Any PoA remains valid.

Collective Account (CAC)

Changes require
- Joint instructions from all duly authorised heirs or executor(s).
- Written consent from the remaining co-holder(s).
- All PoAs get suspended.

💡 Can an existing PoA place orders for an advisory mandate after the client’s death? 

Yes, a Power of Attorney (PoA) may continue to manage the assets of the deceased client and place orders on their behalf. However, all transactions must be carried out solely in the best interests of the heirs and must not compromise the portfolio’s investment strategy.


To ensure adherence to fiduciary responsibilities and regulatory requirements, only specific types of transactions are permitted to be executed after the client’s death, during the period before the heirs or executors are formally confirmed.

✅ Permitted Transactions

    • Roll-over/Renewal of Maturing Instruments
      • Includes fixed-income securities (bonds, notes, certificates of deposit), time deposits, term accounts, structured products or derivatives with capital protection and yield enhancement characteristics
      • Renewals must be conducted using the same or equivalent instrument type, maintaining comparable risk/return profiles.
    • Rating or Universe Downgrades
      • In case of credit rating deterioration or instrument exclusion from eligible universes (e.g. negative view), sell transaction or switches may be proposed and executed within the same class of instruments.
      • Substitutions should maintain similar duration, currency, issuer profile, and risk characteristics.
    • Limited Rebalancing Activities
      • Buy/sell transactions to restore alignment with the portfolio's Strategic Asset Allocation (SAA)  or concentration risks thresholds may be considered.
      • Only justified deviations due to market movements or maturities will be addressed; no tactical repositioning allowed.
    • De-risking Measures following CPR Breaches
      • If a CPR breach occurs de-risking steps may be taken.
      • Actions must remain proportionate and focused on restoring compliance without altering long-term objectives.

❌ Not Permitted Transactions

    • Speculative or strategic shifts that would require a different investment strategy.
    • Investment in new or complex asset categories that were not previously part of the portfolio.
    • Any investment increasing the portfolio investment risk (e.g. Concentration risk)

Any transactions are subject to pre-trade suitability checks (see respective Fallback Guideline) and must be entered via TA10 *. They must also be properly documented in a separate CLM contact note containing the following information: 

        • Heir(s)/executor not yet identified or confirmed
        • Reason for the transaction (e.g. roll-over)
        • Solicitation type
        • Justification of urgency and necessity
        • Reference to supporting documents
        • Written evidence (if no phone logs / records) of PoA confirmation
        • Execution details

* To avoid any assets to be transferred out of the bank, PoA will be suspended in CRM/CLM and with that in WN/DIAS and E-banking.  

💡 Is a PoA permitted to carry out sell transactions to generate liquidity for inheritance-related expenses (e.g. funeral costs, rent, etc.)?


Yes. A Power of Attorney (PoA) is allowed to place sell transactions to cover urgent, inheritance-related expenses (e.g. funeral costs, unpaid rent, or administrative fees). But only if the rightful heirs or executor(s) have not yet been formally identified or confirmed.

Such actions must meet the following criteria:

    • The requested payment is necessary, reasonable, and duly documented (invoice, official notice, or comparable proof).
    • The reason for the sale is plausible and proportionate to the needs of the estate.
    • The transaction is carried out in the best interest of the heirs and does not compromise the overall investment strategy unnecessarily.
    • The RM oversees the entire process and ensures that the proceeds are used strictly for the stated purpose (e.g. by arranging direct payments to the beneficiary or service provider).
    • In case of doubt, the Front may reach out to Legal "Injunctions & Inheritances".
    • Sell transactions must be entered via TA10 (Suitability needs to be checked according respective Fallback Guideline), and properly documented in a separate CLM contact note containing the following information:

      • Heir(s)/executor not yet identified or confirmed
      • Reason for the transaction (e.g. funeral costs)
      • flagged as 'actively solicited'
      • Justification of urgency and necessity
      • Reference to supporting documents
      • Written evidence (if no phone logs / records) of PoA confirmation
      • Execution details

💡 Are other exceptional transactions in financial instrument allowed by the RM within the client's portfoliotransactions

In rare cases involving high risk of material loss and in absence of a PoA, the RM may act independently to protect the portfolio. However, legal guidance must always be sought from the Legal "Injunctions & Inheritances" team prior to any intervention.

 Key considerations before acting:

    • The action serves in the best interest of the heir community (e.g., no action would lead to disproportionate consequences)
    • Full documentation is retained
    • The decision can be justified later to the heirs or appointed executors
    • Prior approval from Legal "Injunctions & Inheritances" was obtained.
    • Any transactions in financial instruments must be entered via TA10  (Suitability needs to be checked according respective Fallback Guideline), and accordingly documented in a separate CLM contact note containing the following information:

      • Heir(s)/executor not yet identified or confirmed
      • Reason for the transaction (e.g. funeral costs)
      • flagged as 'actively solicited'
      • Justification of urgency and necessity
      • Reference to supporting documents
      • Legal basis and approvals by "Injunctions & Inheritances"
      • Execution details


⚠️ Important: Failure to adhere to these guidelines may result in conduct risks or personal/organisational liability exposure.

💡 Can I close portfolio monitoring alerts in the Wealth Navigator?

❌ No. Until the rightful heir(s) or legally appointed executor(s) have been formally identified and verified, any portfolio monitoring alert in the Wealth Navigator can not be closed. This is because the deceased Account Holder’s (including Power of Attorney arrangements) is suspended in CLM and e-banking systems upon notification of death.

✅ Instead:

    • Until the heirs or appointed executors have not officially be confirmed, leave the alerts open in the system, even if they get overdue.
    • Once the legal representative is onboarded and fully verified, communicate the alerts to the authorized person.
    • After closing, submit a standard Advisory Scorecard challenge (Challenge Process) for all previously overdue alerts linked to the deceased client, providing a clear explanation referencing the client’s passing and subsequent verification of heirs.

💡 Can I distributed the Yearly Portfolio Analysis (PAR) report ?

❌ No. Until the rightful heir(s) or legally appointed executor(s) have been formally identified and verified, the PAR must not be distributed via Wealth Navigator. This is because the deceased Account Holder’s (including Power of Attorney arrangements) is suspended in CLM and e-banking systems upon notification of death.

✅ Instead:

    • Do not distribute the PAR until the heirs or appointed executors have officially be confirmed
    • During this interim period, it is acceptable for the PAR alert to remain overdue in the system.
    • Once the legal representative is onboarded and fully verified, distribute the outstanding PAR to the authorized person.
    • After distribution, submit a standard Advisory Scorecard challenge (Challenge Process) linked to the deceased client, providing a clear explanation referencing the client’s passing and subsequent verification of heirs.

💡 Am I allowed to change the portfolio configuration in Wealth Navigator?

❌ No. As long as the rightful heirs or executors have not yet been identified and officially confirmed, no changes to the portfolio configuration should be made in Wealth Navigator.

✅ Instead:

    • Retain the current portfolio configuration until formal instructions are received from the legal heirs or appointed executors. The existing configuration remains valid for administrative, reporting, and compliance purposes during the transitional phase.
    • Once heirs are identified and mandates clarified, reassess portfolio configuration.
    • Ensure all changes are documented in CLM.

💡 How to handle CIP renewal tasks in CLM?

❌ Do not distribute the CIP renewal documents to non confirmed heirs or executors.


✅ Instead:

    • Mark the task as completed in CLM
    • Add a manual contact note in CLM stating:

"CIP renewal completed internally — client deceased. The inheritance process is underway; however, no formal appointment of heirs or executor has yet been received by the bank. Documents not distributed."

💡 Do I need to change the client’s classification after they have passed away?

❌ No. Do not change the deceased client classification

✅ Instead:

    • Retain the current client classification until formal instructions are received from the legal heirs or appointed executors. The existing classification remains valid for administrative, reporting, and compliance purposes during the transitional phase.
    • Once heirs are identified and mandates clarified, reassess classification accordingly.
    • Ensure all changes are documented in CRM.
