HOW DOES THE NEW K&E FORM LOOK LIKE? 

The CIP and the K&E form do no longer need to be signed by the relationship manager ("RM") (i.e. please see the article on How to assess and document K&E of a new client/partner ). The form will be a new worldwide standardized Bank form.


Changes on the CIP:

The CIP and K&E forms are going to be two different forms, nevertheless both are still mandatory for the account holders and need to be completed. In case of a joint/collective accounts, please collect a separate K&E per account holder (and for any other order giver e.g. PoA). Please see see the respective CIP Guidance. The CIP underwent a simplification and re-design (e.g., elimination of the leverage section and cover pages). Please be aware that the CIP and K&E have been removed from the AOF opening package and are now standalone forms to be completed.

The new forms are available in AOF. Nevertheless, it is recommended, wherever possible to use the guided CIP and K&E in CLM for any new client/partner to prevent mistakes during the completion of the form (Link to CLM guidance). For RMLs that offer Trade Basic, the CIP is still not required but the K&E per partner remains mandatory (no change). For the CIP renewal (where applicable), K&E is no longer part of it (Link CIP Renewal). 

For any ESG-related CIP question please refer to the respective separate section in the Suitability WIKI.

WHAT ARE THE MAIN CHANGES?

In the new K&E form particularly the categories Fixed Income, Structured Products and Derivatives have a higher granularity. Additionally, also Digital Assets and Real Estate are included. The categorization in “good”, “limited” and “none” regarding Knowledge and the trading frequency to determine the Experience remains the same:

HOW WILL WEALTH NAVIGATOR CHECK THE NEW K&E CATEGORIES?

Wealth Navigator will support you in checking the K&E level but going forward there will be a distinction in the assessment depending on the instrument. For direct investments (e.g., bonds, stocks etc.), Wealth Navigator checks the corresponding K&E category. For indirect investments (e.g., funds, structured products etc.), Wealth Navigator will check the K&E level in the respective instrument but also the underlying(s).

Example:

  • Direct: For Microsoft Shares -> K&E Category “Equity instruments” is checked
  • Indirect: For a Barrier-Reverse Convertible on Microsoft -> K&E Category “Structured Products – non-principal protected products” and K for “Equity instruments” is checked

How does Wealth Navigator then handle multi-asset class funds? It depends on the fund classification. For multi-asset class funds, where we have sufficient data available, usually the K&E in “Traditional” or “Non-traditional funds” is checked and in addition K for “Equity Instruments” and K for “Investment grade bonds”. There is no further drilldown made. If insufficient data is available, only the category “Traditional” or “Non-traditional funds” is applied.


As of today, depending on the solicitation scenario (solicited or unsolicited) and client’s information on the K&E-form, you might need to warn or educate a client in case the client has limited or no knowledge. You might also need to consider further information in case the client has no trading experience with the instrument in scope (MIFID only). This methodology remains the same (link to K&E alert). Part 4 contains further details on the education process in case of limited or no knowledge.


For RMLs offering Digital Assets:

It is now possible to profile the Knowledge & Experience in Digital Assets of a partner on the K&E form. Please note that this will not replace but complement any existing local processes. Please refer to your local guidance for more details. For RML CH please also see chapter 3 of this guidance for further details regarding the migration clients with existing Digital Assets Waivers.
