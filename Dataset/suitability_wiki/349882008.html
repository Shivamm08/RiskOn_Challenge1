JB Natural Capital Score

https://wiki.juliusbaer.com/spaces/fkh/pages/349882008/JB+Natural+Capital+Score

In short:

The JB Natural Capital Score addresses the topic of biodiversity, air pollution and other pollution, and allows identifying companies with a significant exposure to, and impact on, environmental issues beyond climate. The score averages several relevant data points from both MSCI and Sustainalytics  and classifies issuers in comparison to all issuers in our booked universe.

Short explanation of the calculation:

The base of the Natural capital score, are the natural capital assessments of both MSCI and Sustainalytics. The average of both provider’s assessment is taken. To this average score, deductions are made for PAI indicators relevant to nature, being producing hazardous waste, having hazardous emissions to water as well as having a severe negative on biodiversity sensitive areas. The resulting score is mapped to a JB score, following a logical scale, where a score of 5 or higher results is an average of higher JB score. The score of 3 is reserved for those corporate issuers with a positive contribution to natural capital and biodiversity (which is not possible based on the current status quo data attributes).


Both MSCI and Sustainalytics only provide natural capital relevant scores for the industries its deemed relevant (being the industries with the highest risk and the largest impact on natural capital, such as food production, oil and gas, chemicals, metals and mining). For those corporate issuers for whom no scores are provided, a natural capital score of 0 is assigned, which represents an average or “neutral” score. In this way, a corporate issuer for which the natural capital risk is not deemed relevant, can still become a Sustainable Investment.


Corporate disclosures around natural capital and biodiversity impact are few and relatively immature at this point in time. The MSCI and Sustainalytics scores are therefore still mainly considering the impact of natural capital and biodiversity risk on the financial situation of the corporate issuer in their natural capital relevant scores. Therefore the Natural Capital score is supplemented with data on environmental controversies, limiting the natural capital score in case a corporate issuer has been involved in severe controversies. Additionally, downgrades are made for corporate issuers that derive revenue from economic activities that are deemed particularly harmful for biodiversity and natural capital.
