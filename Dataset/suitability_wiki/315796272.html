Hard block implemented with March 2021 DiAS release for non-institutional investors trying to subscribe the institutional share class of Luxembourg-domiciled funds

2. What is an “institutional client” and a “non-institutional client”?2


As explained on page 8 of the March 2021 DiAS release notes, private individuals (i.e. “non-institutional clients”) are no longer allowed to subscribe the institutional share class of Luxembourg-domiciled funds. Only “institutional clients” are allowed to do this, and this type of clients includes:

  • Clients of discretionary portfolio management mandates;
  • Professional per se and eligible counterparty clients as per MiFID II; and

If the above are considered “institutional clients”, then by exclusion “non-institutional clients” are individuals or legal entities categorized as:

  • Retail; and
  • Elective professional.

Please note that neither the UCITS Directive (i.e. Directive 2009/65/EU) nor the Luxembourgish UCIs Law (i.e. Law of 17 December 2010) are clear with respect to what should be understood by “institutional clients”. Hence the way “institutional and non-institutional clients” are to be understood at BJBE was agreed by the Regulatory Rule Board and the Advisory Rule Board in collaboration with legal advisers.

In addition, please note that “institutional clients” also include accounts where the account holder is an insurance company or an external asset manager (EAMs), since these participants are normally categorized as either eligible counterparties or professionals per se and are considered to be the client.

3. Is the hard block also implemented at other banks in Luxembourg?3

Actually, Luxembourgish law requires not just investment firms (including credit institutions) in Luxembourg but also those all around the world to ensure that only institutional investors can subscribe the institutional share class of Luxembourg-domiciled funds. Even if our competitors apply a more relaxed approach, we (BJBE and our competitors) are all still subject to such requirement.

8. Can I allow a non-institutional client holding units/shares in the institutional share class of a Luxembourg-domiciled fund to make a “traspaso” to another fund?

Yes, as long as the “traspaso” is made to a share class for which the non-institutional client is eligible (i.e. in the case of Luxembourg-domiciled funds, the non-institutional share class), and the client is an individual (i.e. not a legal entity) and a tax resident in Spain.

The concept of “traspaso” (“transfer” in English) from one fund to another is a purely fiscal concept.

From an operational perspective, a “traspaso” between fund A and fund B works the same way as redeeming fund A and using the received cash to subscribe fund B.

However, from a tax perspective in Spain, when an investor makes a “traspaso” from fund A and fund B, if he/she/it has made a profit in fund A before making the “traspaso”, he/she/it defers having to pay taxes to the moment of the final redemption.

A “traspaso” is simply a fiscal tool in Spain to allow investors to keep their money in the “fund system” and to defer having to pay taxes for any profits made to the moment when they bring their money back to the “cash system” with the final redemption.

9. Can I allow a non-institutional client to make a “traspaso” to the institutional share class of a Luxembourg-domiciled fund?

No, because the non-institutional client is not eligible to subscribe the institutional share class of Luxembourg-domiciled funds.

11. How should I clearly document trades?

When you document trades on DiAS, you have to ensure that you clearly define the following three variables.

  • The financial instrument to which the trade relates.


  • Initiation:
    • In case of advice, who initiated the provision of advice (Initiation = Client/Bank)
    • In case of no advice, that the trade was initiated by the client (Initiation = Client)


  • Client feedback:
    • In case of advice and:
      • Initiation = Bank, whether it was accepted or rejected (Client feedback = Accepted/Rejected)
      • Initiation = Client, whether the client agreed to proceed with it or withdrew (Client feedback = Proceed with advice / Client withdraws)
    • In case of no advice, that no advice was provided (Client feedback = Proceed without advice)

Let’s look at examples of trades in each of the solicitation scenarios and how you should document them on DiAS:


  • Unsolicited


Example: I call my client to ask him to update his K&E. He asks me to send him the K&E form and says he will fill it out, sign it, scan it and email it back to me. Then he asks me, now that he has me on the phone, to buy 500 shares of Apple. I execute the order.

We go through it together and I keep the information I give to him on the ESG financial instruments mentioned in the marketing material strictly factual. However, the client then asks me to provide him with advice on which of them to buy based on his personal situation (overall risk profile, investment objectives and investment strategy). I then start an advisory session, advise him to buy shares in one of the mentioned financial instruments (AALBERTS NV), and the client ends up following my advice.

Overall investment strategy vs. CIP investment strategy overnight check12

12. On DiAS, I can find the overall investment strategy rating per portfolio, but not the overall investment strategy weighted rating per account. Is there a way I can check it? If yes, how?

The overall investment strategy weighted rating is not displayed anywhere by DiAS. Of course, DiAS calculates it constantly and, as explained in the training, it automatically triggers an alert when it is above the range permitted by the CIP investment strategy (hence you will for sure know its value is greater than the maximum value allowed by the CIP investment strategy range). However, even if it does not display its value at a specific time, you may calculate it manually.


ETD interim process13

13. Do I have to send my client a cost disclosure even if I have a flat fee agreement with him/her?

Yes. In accordance with MiFID II, you also have to send your client an “empty” cost disclosure. Please check with your client if he/she/it agrees with going forward with the ETD yearly special process. In the email that you send to your client, you should include an explanation of why he/she/it will receive the cost disclosure even in the case of a flat fee having been agreed.14

14. If I am using the ETD yearly special process, can I send the cost disclosure and the PRIIP KID to my client via eBanking?

As your client has to reconfirm in writing to the yearly process we recommend to send an e-mail with the Cost Disclosure and KID/PRIIPS to your client.

Please also remember the proper TAP documentation and archive the documents in FIO.15

15. If my client is categorized as professional, do I have to send a generic PRIIP KID and a cost disclosure to him/her, too?

If your client is categorized as elective professional or professional per se:

  • You don’t have to send him/her/it a generic PRIIP KID; but
  • You have to send him/her/it the cost disclosure.

Please remember that you must provide a PRIIP KID to EU-domiciled retail clients before they buy a financial instrument that qualifies as a PRIIP. This means that, if your client is categorized as retail but is not domiciled in the EU, you can but you are not required to send him a PRIIP KID pre-trade.16

16. Are Toolbox products also affected by the ETD yearly special process?

No. All Toolbox products (e.g. FX forwards, structured products) traded via FX-, FX Option- and Derivative Toolbox are not affected by this special process.

This process only applies to exchange traded derivatives (ETDs), that is, options and futures traded on an exchange, like EUREX or CME.17

17. DiAS generates a cost disclosure already, so why is it not sufficient?

Unfortunately, DiAS is only capable of automatically generating “empty” cost disclosures for trades in ETDs.



For the time being, an enhancement so DiAS automatically generates cost disclosures is not planned. Hence you have to manually generate and send them to your client.

18. To whom should I provide the mandatory pre-trade documentation in case of dealing with an intermediary?

As a rule, you must provide the mandatory pre-trade documentation (PRIIP KID, cost disclosure, suitability report) to the account holder, who is contractually our client.

However, the specific documents you will have to provide to the client will in the end depend on his/her/its MiFID II category (retail / professional / eligible counterparty). For instance, if the client is categorized as retail and is EU-domiciled, then you will have to provide him/her/it with the PRIIP KID.

Now, let’s take a look at the pre-contractual documentation you will have to provide depending on the type of account set-up.


Insurance companies

  1. In accounts participated by an insurance company (as account holder) and the policy holder (as policy holder):
  • Client = Insurance company
  • The pre-contractual documentation provision rules you must follow are:
    • Cost disclosure to be provided to the insurance company, and
    • PRIIP KID and suitability report not to be provided to the insurance company (since insurance companies are categorized as either professional per se or eligible counterparty).


2. In accounts participated by an insurance company (as PoA) and the policy holder (as account holder) – Finnish model:

  • Client = Insurance company (since it is the owner of the assets being managed)
  • The pre-contractual documentation provision rules you must follow are:
    • (Same as in case 1 above),
    • However, in this special account set-up, the mandatory pre-trade documentation (PRIIP KID, cost disclosure, suitability report) is provided to the policy holder to grant him/her/it with the highest level of MiFID II protection.


EAMs

3. In accounts participated by an EAM (as PoAs) and the end investor (as account holder):

  • Client = End investor
  • The pre-contractual documentation provision rules you must follow are:
    • If the EAM is not providing the end investor with discretionary portfolio management and BJBE is the PRIIP manufacturer, PRIIP KID to be made available to the EAM (who will then distribute it further to the client if needed), and
    • Cost disclosure and suitability report not to be provided neither to the end investor nor to the EAM, since the EAM is itself an investment firm (i.e. it is licensed under MiFID II), so EAM itself will directly perform the pre-trade suitability test and provide by itself these pre-contractual documents to the end investor.


Trusts

4. In accounts participated by a trustee (as account holder) and the trust beneficiary (as the entitled person):

  • Client = Trustee
  • The pre-contractual documentation provision rules you must follow are:
    • PRIIP KID to be provided to the trustee if the trustee is EU-domiciled and retail,
    • Suitability report to be provided to the trustee if the trustee is retail, and
    • Cost disclosure to be provided to the trustee in any case.


Other PoAs

5. In accounts participated by family offices (as PoA) and the end investor (as account holder):

  • Client = End investor
  • The pre-contractual documentation provision rules you must follow are:
    • PRIIP KID to be provided to the end investor if the end investor is EU-domiciled and retail; may be provided to the family office (or other PoA) if authorized by the end investor,
    • Suitability report to be provided to the end investor if the end investor is retail; may be provided to the family office (or other PoA) if authorized by the end investor, and
    • Cost disclosure to be provided to the end investor in any case; may be provided to the family office (or other PoA) if authorized by the end investor.


Clear documentation of calls with clients19

19. Why am I now required to indicate whether phone calls with clients were held via JB landline or JB cell phone line?

Because there are two different platforms where phone calls get recorded – phone calls via JB landline get recorded on one and phone calls via JB cell phone line get recorded on another. To ensure that we can at all times easily locate and retrieve all phone calls, this indication is fundamental.

Other questions22

22. Do bonds qualify as PRIIPs?

Some types of bonds do and others don’t. For a clear indication of which types qualify as PRIIPs, please refer to the Joint ESA Supervisory Statement on the application of scope of the PRIIPs Regulation to bonds. Of course, if a bond qualifies as a PRIIP, and the client is retail and EU-domiciled, then the bond can only be bought by the client if a PRIIP KID can be given to them as pre-trade documentation.
