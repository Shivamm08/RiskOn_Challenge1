EMIR - Questions & Answers

https://wiki.juliusbaer.com/spaces/fkh/pages/540806325/EMIR+-+Questions+Answers


1.To which clients does EMIR apply?

EMIR applies to all undertakings established in the European Economic Area (“EEA”, which includes European Union Member States plus Liechtenstein, Iceland and Norway) that trade derivatives (both over-the-counter (“OTC”) and exchange-traded derivatives (“ETD”)). The term "undertaking" is not limited to legal entities, but encompasses every company, foundation, family office, trust, or individual that engages in "economic activity" (i.e., trades in goods and/or services). Individuals acting in their own capacity (i.e. in their role as private persons), and third-country entities (e.g. UK undertakings) are not subject to EMIR. However, they may still be required to comply with certain EMIR requirements when dealing with BJBE. Entities subject to EMIR must report details of any derivatives contracts entered into, as well as any modifications or terminations of such contracts, to a registered or recognized trade repository by the end of the next working day.

2. How is your client classified under EMIR?

Undertakings qualify as “counterparties”, which are classified into the following categories:

  • "Financial Counterparty" (“FC”), i.e. an entity professionally involved in financial markets, such as:

- Investment firm,
- Credit institution,
- Insurance undertaking or reinsurance undertaking,
- UCITS and, where relevant, its management company,
- Institution for occupational retirement provision,
- Alternative investment fund (“AIF”),
- Central securities depository, and

  • "Non-Financial Counterparty" (“NFC”), i.e. an undertaking established in the European Union other than a financial counterparty or a central counterparty (“CCP”) or a trade repository.

The status of:

  • Of an FC can be “FC+” or “FC-”, and
  • Of an NFC can be “NFC+” or “NFC-”, depending on whether their OTC derivatives positions are above or below the clearing thresholds set by the European Securities and Markets Authority (“ESMA”).


2.1. In practice, how do clients get classified as per EMIR?

The assessment of which EMIR category your client belongs to must be carried out by your client itself based on its overall derivatives exposure, and not only the volume traded with BJBE, via form 1-1276 (“EMIR Addendum to the Master Agreement”).

2.2. What about non-EEA clients?

The above classification also applies to non-EEA clients, which are required to fill out with form 1-1276 (“EMIR Addendum to the Master Agreement”) the EMIR clearing status (+/-) that would apply to them if they were established in the EEA .

3. Does your client have to obtain a Legal Entity Identifier ("LEI") to comply with the reporting obligation under EMIR?

Under EMIR, the "no LEI, no trade" rule applies.

EMIR stipulates that legal entity counterparties obtain a Legal Entity Identifier (“LEI”) for reporting purposes.

To comply with this requirement, the client must:

  • Provide a valid LEI in a timely manner;
  • Renew their LEI regularly;
  • Inform BJBE promptly of any changes to their classification (e.g., from NFC- to NFC+), including any updates to their LEI in the event of a corporate restructuring, etc.

4. What about trusts - do they need to obtain a LEI?

A trust is an arrangement where a person or legal entity (the trustee) holds assets as the nominal owner for the benefit of one or more beneficiaries. These assets are typically held in a special purpose vehicle and are considered to constitute a separate legal entity.

As a result, trusts are obliged to obtain a LEI for reporting and regulatory purposes.

5. Trades in which products are subject to EMIR?

All derivatives trades (both in ETDs and OTC derivatives) are subject to EMIR.

An “OTC derivative (contract)” refers to a derivative contract that is executed outside of a regulated market or a third-country market deemed equivalent to a regulated market.

Examples of OTC derivatives include:

  • FX forwards;
  • FX swaps;
  • OTC options;
  • Interest rate swaps;
  • ACDC.

Please note that spot transactions with settlement periods of two days or less (T+2 or shorter) do not qualify as OTC derivatives.

On the other hand, “ETDs” are standardized, non-securitized contracts that are executed on organized markets.

Examples of ETDs include:

  • Futures contracts.
  • Options contracts on an equity index underlying.

6. Which EMIR-related forms does your client need to sign?

Two forms are relevant:

  • Form 1-1276 (“EMIR Addendum to the Master Agreement”): This form is mandatory – it must be signed by all clients (both existing and new, regardless of location and including natural persons) who wish to trade derivatives. Clients must sign this form and provide their relevant data, such as classification and LEI code. This is so that BJBE can comply with its own reporting obligation under EMIR.
  • Form 1-1158 (“EMIR Reporting Service Agreement”): This form is optional but highly recommended because, with it, the client delegates their reporting obligation under EMIR to BJBE (and hence does not need to comply with it themselves). The client should sign it in the following situations:
    • When an EEA client classified as FC+, FC-, or NFC+ wants to delegate both Exchange-Traded Derivatives (ETDs) and OTCs reporting to BJBE.
    • When an EEA client classified as NFC- wants to delegate ETDs reporting to BJBE. (Please note that this form is not necessary for NFC- clients trading OTC derivatives, as BJBE is already required to report such trades on their behalf.)

The client must also sign other forms which are not related to EMIR in order to trade OTC derivatives and ETDs. Please refer to Q&A #11 for further details.

7. What is the consequence if your client does not sign form 1-1276?

If your client does not sign form 1-1276 (“EMIR Addendum to the Master Agreement”), they will be unable to trade products that fall within the scope of EMIR (please refer to Q&A #5 for details).

8. What is the consequence if your client does not sign form 1-1158?

If your client does not sign form 1-1158 (“EMIR Reporting Service Agreement”), they will still be permitted to trade products governed by EMIR. However, you must inform them that they will be responsible for handling their own EMIR reporting (with all the risks and disadvantages that such a “self-reporting” might imply). 

9. What process do you have to follow after your client signs form 1-1158?

Please inform your client that, because by signing form 1-1158 they have delegated their EMIR reporting obligation to BJBE, they are now required to confirm to “Regis TR” (i.e. BJBE’s trade repository) this delegation agreement.

As part of this confirmation process:

  • Clients will receive an email from RegisTR to the email address indicated in form 1-1158, containing a link to confirm the delegation (“Manage delegations” > “Accept delegation” > “Confirm”). Please refer to the “E-Mail registration by client” section of this procedure);
  • Clicking on the link within that email is necessary to finalize the delegation.

In case the client is not acting on this e-mail, he will receive a weekly reminder.
In case the client is not acting on the reminder, the RMs will be informed by the Derivatives Processing Team.

N.B.! Please bear in mind that until the client completes the delegation confirmation process, the client remains responsible for the reporting of the trades executed in the meantime.

10. What do you have to do if your client has not yet confirmed the delegation agreement and would like to provide another email address?

If your client has not yet confirmed the delegation and asks you to change the email address previously provided, please note that any changes to an email address for EMIR reporting purposes must be submitted using a new form 1-1158 (EMIR Reporting Service Agreement).

11. Does your client have to sign other forms to trade OTC derivatives and ETDs?

Yes, the respective ones listed on this overview.

Please remember that form 1-1131 (“Master Agreement for Financial Derivatives Transactions “OTC”) and form 1-1156 (“Master Agreement for Trading in Financial Forward Transactions “ETD”) are also mandatory documents, although they are not directly related to EMIR. These forms serve as the foundation of BJBE's contractual framework for clients seeking to trade OTC and ETD derivatives.
