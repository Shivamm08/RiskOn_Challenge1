What is "Suitable advice"?


“Suitable advice” in short means advising clients

  1. in line with their investment strategy and risk profile,
  2. with products they are eligible to buy according to their client characteristics,
  3. with products they understand well and
  4. with products that we are permitted to recommend (offered universe).

Suitability checks are complex and consider elements such as the , investment strategy, and the characteristics of the proposed product, to name a few. Fortunately, all these checks are integrated into DiAS/Wealth Navigator and client-facing staff only need to ensure that the alerts resulting from these checks are duly handled in an advisory session BEFORE advice is provided.

By opening an advisory session and running the checks before making recommendations to a client, client-facing staff can not only check if their advice is in line with the client’s investment strategy, but also find out if there are any restrictions in selling certain products (e.g. certain fund share classes) to their client, even on an unsolicited basis.

This article describes what elements must be considered to provide suitable advice, explains when advice is not suitable and provides further references to selected key topics.

Which elements must be considered for suitable advice?

  • Client suitability: Assessment whether the recommended financial instrument(s) is suitable and/or appropriate for the client, based on information about the client:
    • Is the client a retail or a professional client?
    • Investment objectives: What are the client's investment goals, risk tolerance, and time horizon?
    • Risk tolerance and financial situation: How much risk is the client willing and able to take on?
    • Investment strategy: Is the investment strategy in line with client’s overall investment strategy evaluated in the CIP?
    • Does the client have sufficient knowledge and experience to understand the financial instrument being offered?
    • Other client attributes like client domicile and tax status
  • Product suitability: Is a particular financial instrument suitable for the client, based on its characteristics, selling / distribution restrictions, target market, costs and risks? Is the financial instrument in line with the view of our JB experts and investment universe status?

Another aspect to consider is the service promise that the bank contractually agrees with the client in the advisory contract of each portfolio (e.g. respecting SAA, concentration risks or the agreed bond rating threshold).

When is advice not suitable?

If any transaction or set of transactions is not in line with any checked element on portfolio-level and therefore not suitable, a business-critical alert is triggered in DiAS/Wealth Navigator. The following principles must be followed:

Active solicitation: Any advice which is actively provided to a client must not lead to a business-critical alert, e.g. no SAA breach, no BMO hard block etc. Therefore, an advisory session must be started with the suitability checks run before advising the client. There are very few permitted exceptions, which can be found here (Link).  Consolidated Product Risk alerts might be triggered when advising derivatives for hedging purposes. For this dedicated case “Methodology Exception” may be used to justify (including a detailed documentation in the contact note why the CPR is still in line).

Note: If there is any actively solicited transaction in a session, every other transaction in the same advisory session must be suitable as well (→ no portfolio business-critical alerts such as CPR, SAA or Issuer Concentration). See also section below.

Reverse solicitation: Any advice provided on an instrument on client request must not lead to a business-critical alert. In case of a business-critical alert, the client must be advised against the transaction. If the client still wants to go ahead, the client’s risk acceptance and the rationale why must be documented (dedicated dropdowns can be used to simplify documentation: Link).

“Unrelated alerts” – why is an alert appearing on a position which was not part of the advice?

Threshold-based alerts (e.g. SAA, Concentration risks etc.) are usually calculated on the overall gross market value of the portfolio. Negative values, e.g. a negative cash account, are usually considered as 0. In such cases, if a position is sold, the gross market value of a portfolio may decrease:

All transactions in the session are considered when running the suitability checks, therefore the new (lower) portfolio value is used in the calculation of the portfolio-level checks. Hence, another position already included in a portfolio could become relatively larger and breach another threshold.

Alerts in such a case may seem “unrelated” to the transaction(s) proposed in the session. However, they are not – the advice must be suitable from a portfolio perspective instead of “only” from the single transaction(s). Please note, in case such an alert appears for unsolicited transactions only, you must warn the client and close the alert with "defer" (please document the warning in the contact note that you can refer to this contact note when the overnight alert appears the following days). 

Mix of different solicitation types:mixedsolicitation 

It can happen that one advisory session includes a suitable instrument which is actively solicited and in addition, another instrument requested by the client on unsolicited basis. As the active solicitation is the master, all other transactions – including the unsolicited one - must be suitable, which might lead to a business-critical portfolio alert. There are two ways to approach this situation:

  1. Remove initially advised product or amend the investment amount
  2. Add an additional transaction to rebalance the portfolio that the overall composition is suitable again.

Documentation:

Always document what you have discussed with the client, including all provided warnings, education etc. Please also make clear for each transaction in writing whether you have provided advice or not. In cases of phone conversations or meetings, a detailed client contact note must be created to capture the context and content of the discussion. By doing so, we can ensure that all client interactions are properly documented and easily accessible for future reference. Further information on how to document in DiAS/Wealth Navigator can be found here.
