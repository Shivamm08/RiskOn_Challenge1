1. Creating a Resolution Note (in the client book) for scenarios with justification type "client accepts risk"

In case of a single resolution note (use the check Action symbol [ ] in the line of the respective alert), the justification rationale are available to support documentation (→Justification rationale (dropdown values) for "client accepts risk").

However, if you are using “justify all” (if available) to close one or several alerts with “client accepts risk”, please note 1) the risks that have been explained, 2) the client’s decision after the explanation and 3) the client's rationale in the contact note.

Consolidated Product Risk

1. I notified the account holder by email that portfolio CPR is higher than the mandate risk budget. He should either reduce positions with a high product risk rating, or consider changing the investment strategy if he is comfortable with this level of risk. I did not provide advice on individual financial instruments.

2. He confirmed today by phone that he wants to hold the positions and does not want to change the strategy. We agreed to review the situation again in six months.

1. I had the annual portfolio review with the account holder on the phone.  I informed him that the CPR exceeds the risk budget for his “Income” strategy. He invests mainly in fixed income and has a specific yield target for each bond. As yields have decreased and some bonds matured, reinvestments have been made into less liquid junk bonds. I recommended to sell Bond X and equity Y to decrease CPR.

2. He wants to hold, but considers changing the strategy to Balanced. We agreed to snooze the alert for a month.

The client was informed that the portfolio risk is higher than what their investment strategy permits. The client acknowledged this, but did not want to make changes to [existing positions / new orders] as they are comfortable with the risk.

Concentration Risk on Single Positions

1. Account holder Mr. X called me to place some unsolicited buy orders. I informed him that exposure to the JB Next Generation fund is 21% of the portfolio, which has triggered a concentration risk. I advised him to reduce the position to 15%.

2. He would like to hold the position, as it is a long-term conviction holding. He agreed to review the situation again in six months.

1. I met with the Authorized Signatory Mrs X on April 16th for a portfolio review. I told her that the portfolio is very concentrated in Zoom Communications (22%) and Nvidia (25%), due last year’s strong performance. I advised to sell Nvidia, as it’s rated Reduce by JB. She asked for my opinion on Zoom, and I informed that I cannot give advice as the stock is not covered.

2. She agreed to sell Nvidia and reduce Zoom to 15%. As she does not want to be notified of Zoom again the alert was snoozed for 12 months.

The client was informed that the transaction in [instrument] results in a concentration risk in a single position. The client acknowledged this but decided to proceed with the order as they are comfortable with the risk.

Strategic Asset Allocation

1. I had an annual portfolio review meeting with the account holder where I informed her that the portfolio is not in line with the SAA of the mandate strategy (CHF Balanced). I therefore suggested to reduce equities by 10 %-points and increase the reference currency weight by 10 %-points.

2. She told me that she is happy to hold this allocation for the time being , as she believes the US equity market will keep trending higher. She agreed to review the situation again in three months.


1. The PoA holder called to ask for JB’s equity recommendation in the consumer staples sector. I told we recommend buying Nestlé, but his portfolio is already overexposed to equities (Balanced strategy with 65% equity allocation). I advised him to sell Lockheed Martin (that he bought earlier, against advice) to bring the asset allocation back in line.

2. He decided to keep Lockheed and bought Nestlé with 5%, accepting the equity overexposure. We agreed to review the Asset Allocation again in six months.


The client was informed that the allocation to [asset class] is [above / below] the weights of their investment strategy. The client acknowledged this, but did not want to make changes to [existing positions / new orders] as they are comfortable with the allocation.

Negative View

1. The account holder called my ARM and wanted to buy three new stocks. The portfolio has existing positions in Square Inc. and Tesla, which JB research recommends to reduce. My ARM informed her of our recommendations and advised to sell both.

2. She decided to sell Square, but against advice decided to increase the position in Tesla. She said investors are too pessimistic about Tesla’s future prospects, as it is not only a car company, but also an energy company, and that she believes in Elon Musk.

1. I informed the authorized signatory that JB research has issued a sell rating on Turkey government bonds, implicating potential downside. The portfolio is exposed to one TRY-denominated bond and one USD-denominated bond. I advised her to sell both.

2. She agreed to sell the TRY bond but wanted to hold the USD bond for the time being. We agreed to review the situation again in one month.

The client was informed that [instrument] is rated [sell / reduce]. The client did not want to sell the position.

Bond Rating Threshold

1. I informed the account holder by email that the Bubble Finance PLC -2069 bond is downgraded to BB-, which is below the portfolio minimum threshold of BBB-.

2. He confirmed today over the phone that he wants to keep the position. He also noted that he is interested in increasing his exposure in lower rated USD-denominated bonds. Client agreed to reduce the bond threshold of the portfolio from BBB-to B.

1. The account holder called me and asked for a 1m CHF Lombard loan. During the call I told him that the Republic of Jamaica bond he transferred from another Bank is rated B+. The minimum bond threshold of his portfolio is BBB-. I informed him that the minimum bond threshold can be changed if he wants to hold bonds with a lower rating and accepts the risk.

2. He confirmed that he wants to hold the position, without changing the threshold and requested not to be notified for 12 months, unless it worsens.

The client was informed that [instrument] is rated below the portfolio’s minimum bond rating threshold. The client did not want to sell the position.

Issuer Concentration Risk

1. I contacted the account holder as her portfolio has a 22% exposure to bonds issued by Boeing Co. I informed her of the risk that this concentration entails and recommended to sell the bond with the longest time to maturity.

2. She wanted to hold the positions against my advice and said she is comfortable with the risk. She asked if it would be possible not to be notified of concentrations below 30%. I said that the threshold can be changed to 30% upon her request and she asked me to do so.

1. I notified the account holder by email that his portfolio has a 30% concentration to Structured Products issued by BNP Paribas. These positions have been transferred to us from his previous bank. The client is very self-directed, so I asked what he wants to do and did not provide advice on any of the instruments.

2. The account holder decided to sell one position, which decreased the exposure to 25%. He informed me he wants to hold the remaining ones until maturity. He agreed to review again in 12 months.

The client was informed that the transaction in [instrument] results in an issuer concentration risk. The client acknowledged this but decided to proceed as they are comfortable with the risk.

Overall Investment Strategy 

Already existing mandate

1. I informed the client that the investment strategy 'Capital Gain' of their mandate is higher than the strategy of the CIP 'balanced' and explained the risk that this may lead to the fact that higher gains are possible but also higher losses. I therefore recommended to change the strategy of this mandate to 'balanced' and offered a new contract.

2. The client understands and acknowledged this, but did not want to make any changes of the strategy nor the mandate, because of their core-satellite approach in conjunction with portfolio 01.02 under the same ZR/BR. 

New mandate setup

1. I recommended to the client the discretionary mandate with investment strategy 'balanced' as this strategy fits to the one elaborated in the CIP. The client wanted to invest in this mandate but with the strategy 'Capital Gain'. I explained to the client that 'Capital Gain' is higher than the calculated investment strategy of the CIP and I therefore do not recommend to choose this strategy, as there are higher gains expected but also higher losses which does not fit to the gathered client investment profile.

2. The client acknowledged my explanation and wants to invest even though in the DM with Capital Gain, as she/he has other advisory- and discretionary mandates with other banks and from a holistic perspective over all portfolios with all banks, the aggregated overall investment strategy for her/him is well balanced. 

The Client Investment Profile has a lower strategy than the strategy of the mandate

I informed the client that the investment strategy 'Capital Gain' of their mandate is higher than the strategy of the CIP 'balanced' and explained the risk that this may lead to the fact that higher gains are possible but also higher losses. The client understands and acknowledged this, but did not want to make any immediate changes, because of the core-satellite approach in conjunction with portfolio 01.02 under the same ZR/BR.
