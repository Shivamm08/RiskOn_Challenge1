Overview

Fund Selling Restrictions are restrictions and requirements defined by the fund provider in the fund prospectus. They prohibit the distribution of particular share classes in certain contexts, make it dependent on the fulfilment of certain conditions and/or restrict the circle of eligible investors.

Frequent restrictions are, for example:

  • the requirement of a distribution agreement (between fund provider and distributor)
  • the requirement of a fee agreement (e.g. Advisory or Discretionary mandate) 
  • the requirement of a certain legal classification of the investor (e.g. classification as Luxembourg institutional investor).

Categories of restrictions

  • Country Restrictions: Client or Distributor (RM) must not be located in particular countries
  • Distributor Circle Restrictions: Share class may only be promoted by selected distribution channels (e.g. only by distributors that have a distribution agreement with the fund company)
  • Requirement of a fee agreement: Investor needs an advisory or a discretionary mandate with the distributor 
  • Investor Circle Restrictions: Investor circle is limited to specific investors as defined by the fund provider in the prospectus (e.g. to pension fund companies, collective investment vehicles, affiliated persons of the fundhouse etc.)
  • Investor Type Restrictions: Share class is limited to a specific investor type as required by the legal fund structure (e.g. QIF, SIF), tax (e.g. taxe d’abonnement) and/or prospectus in the corresponding fund domicile. (Examples: limited to Luxembourg institutional investors according to Art. 174 para. 2 letter c of the Act of 17 December 2010, Swiss qualified investors, UK sophisticated investors, institutional investors as defined in fund prospectus, etc.)
  • Investor Prerequisites: Specific terms and conditions have to be complied with before investing in the share class type (e.g. prior approval of the fund manager is needed)
  • Contract Requirement: Share class requires a contractual set-up with the fund company before investments are done  

Information on retro-free funds

One of the BMO fund selling restrictions is the restriction to invest into retro-free funds: 

BMO Messages: 

“To invest in this share class, the client needs to be in an agreement which does not permit Julius Baer to receive and retain inducements (i.e. discretionary mandate)”

“This share class must not be sold by Julius Baer as it is limited to advisory agreements which do not permit the distributor to receive and retain inducements.”

Rationale for this rule (by Legal Investment Solutions & Distribution): 

The purpose of this BMO Alert Message is to ensure compliance with the respective fund selling restriction which only permits investments by clients which concluded a written retro-free service model with the Bank.

These fund selling restrictions apply regardless of the specific solicitation scenario. As the Bank in principle does not offer any retro-free execution only or advisory service models, the share class is not open for investments for clients in these service models (only open for investments in discretionary scenarios). Stringent adherence to these selling restrictions ensures that the Bank’s clients are treated equally.


Information on Investor Type Restrictions- relevant for limitations to Institutional share classes (special attention to LUX and IRL funds)

Another BMO restriction is highlighting the Institutional share classes.

BMO Messages are e.g.:

"To invest in this share class the client needs to be an institutional investor according to Luxembourg law or a discretionary mandate client. For further information please consult the prospectus."

"To invest in this share class, the client needs to be an institutional investor as per art. 174 para. 2 letter c of the 2010 Act."

Carefully read below principles to be followed without any exceptions to define if the client can be considered eligible for this share class: 

  1. MIFID Eligible Counterparty, MiFID Per Se, FIDLEG Institutional, FIDLEG Per Se are allowed to buy Institutional share classes.
  2. MiFID elected professionals, MiFID retail, FILDEG elected professionals and FIDLEG private are not allowed to buy Institutional share classes.
  3. Leeway: There is no leeway that we can use to allow non-eligible clients (see 2. above) to access share classes that are reserved for Institutional share classes regardless of the trade size.
  4. The negative consequences for LUX funds could be non-compliance with the tax regime (reduced tax d’abonnement 0.01% vs. 0.05%) due to contaminated Institutional share classes with non-eligible investors. This would translate into potential regulatory and contractual litigation risks a) CSSF vs ManCo, b) ManCo/fund vs. distribution partner c) ManCo/fund vs. transfer agent.
  5. With the exception of the taxation relieve, the same issues can be articulated for IRL regulated funds as potential non-compliance with the defined target investor audience per specific share class triggers potential regulatory and contractual litigation risks.
  6. In locations where the client classifications under 1+2 are not rolled out (see: Global Client Classification Logic (MiFID vs. FIDLEG) - Suitability Wiki - Confluence (juliusbaer.com)) the following clients may be treated as equivalent to the client classifications under 1 and invest in Institutional share classes:

-        banks

-        securities/investment firms

-        supranational organisations

-        insurance companies

-        public entities, institutions and foundations with professional treasury operations*

-        private investment structures with professional treasury operations created for high-net-worth retail clients*

-        managers of collective assets

-        portfolio managers

-        fund management companies

-        large companies that reach at least two of the following thresholds:

(i)      balance sheet of CHF/EUR 20 million (or equivalent);

(ii)     turnover of CHF/EUR 40 million (or equivalent); or

(iii)    equity of CHF/EUR 2 million (or equivalent).

-        trustees

-        collective investment schemes

-        centrals banks

-        pension funds and their management companies

-        national governments and corresponding offices

-        companies with professional treasury operations*

* ”Professional treasury operations” means that - on a permanent basis - the management of the assets are entrusted to a professionally qualified person with experience in the financial sector.

Information on US Fund Selling Restrictions (released on Sept 2025)

One of the BMO fund selling restrictions is implemented to ensure compliance with US securities laws by providing clear transparency and enforcing hard blocks on fund investments for clients with US Nexus. 


When is the alert triggered:

For DiAS / WN Cases ONLY:

  • If Tax Profile has “US Person” and the Product Attribute "usRestrictionType" is "US Citizen" or "Unclear" => Alert in DiAS / WN
  • If Tax Profile has “US Person” and the Product Attribute "usRestrictionType" is NEITHER "US Citizen" NOR "Unclear" => No Alert

For DiAS / WN and PSP cases:

  • If Tax Profile does NOT have “US Person” and the Product Attribute "usRestrictionType" is "US Citizen" or "Unclear" => Action Required in DiAS / WN / PSP
  • If Tax Profile does NOT have “US Person” and the Product Attribute "usRestrictionType" is NEITHER "US Citizen" NOR "Unclear" => No Alert


BMO Message: 

“The share class is subject to US Selling Restrictions, incl. US nationals non-resident in the US / non-entities subject to US taxation (W-9 documentation for QI purposes).”


Rationale for this rule: 

  • In compliance with US securities laws and regulations, fund providers establish and enforce strict sales restrictions to adhere to applicable regulatory standards. To ensure adherence to these sales restrictions new functionalities to provide transparency around fund sales restrictions to clients with US Nexus will be implemented
  • Implementation of U.S fund selling restrictions via a new BMO rule in DiAS / WN. For all Advisory Service Models, transactions of clients with US Nexus who want to buy restricted funds will be blocked (data exceptions possible).
  • BMO information to be reflected in PSP for PMs to consider restrictions when buying holdings into Discretionary Mandates.
