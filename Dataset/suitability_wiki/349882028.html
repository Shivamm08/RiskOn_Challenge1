MSCI ESG Rating

The MSCI ESG ratings are designed to measure a company’s resilience to long-term, industry material environmental, social and governance (ESG) risks. It is a qualitative rating based on self-reported information of companies, government data, some academic journals, and media publications. The rating is calculated by analysts of MSCI and evaluates the ESG performance of a company in comparision to its peers in that industry. The ratings range from leader (AAA, AA), average (A, BBB, BB) to laggard (B, CCC). As a reminder, if a company is rated CCC by MSCI (representing the bottom 5% of companies in that sub-industry), it is considered an ESG risk at JB.

MSCI identifies six to ten key ESG issues for each sub-industry according to the Global Industry Classification Standard (there are 158 sub-industries in total). Company-specific issues can also be identified when special controversies exist which require a deeper monitoring of other issues. Companies are then scored based on the key issues in that industry. Those scores are then normalized and the ESG rating is provided based on the performance of the companies in relation to its peers in the same sub-industry.

MSCI Controversy Flags

The MSCI Controversy Flag signals whether a corporate issuer has a notable controversy (or controversies) related to a company’s operations and/or products, and the severity of the social or environmental impact of the controversy. Controversies include:

  • Violations of existing laws or regulations the company is subject to,
  • Company actions or events that violate commonly accepted international norms,
  • Controversies in the supply-chain (unsafe working conditions, inadequate pay, etc.); and
  • Controversial sourcing of raw material with a substantial environmental impact
