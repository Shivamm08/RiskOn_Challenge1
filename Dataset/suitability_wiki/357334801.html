In the last few years, the term Greenwashing has become an important Buzzword in the industry. Yet regardless of its frequent use, currently no generally accepted legal definition exists. Despite this lack of definition, some regulators have provided guidance about what Greenwashing is and how to avoid it. In general, Greenwashing must be addressed in a Bank on Entity, Financial Product/Service and Point of Sale levels:

  1. At entity level: Greenwashing at entity level happens when a company markets itself as sustainable in documentation such as in social media, advertising, the publication of statements of support, in annual reports or in sustainability reports.
  2. At product/service level: Greenwashing at financial product or service level happens, for example, when products or services:
  • are called “Impact” or “Net-Zero” without measuring or disclosing how this is achieved;
  • are sold as “sustainable” despite containing underlying assets which do significant harm to the environment;
  • declare strategies such as norm-based integration of ESG criteria, but don’t actually use these strategies;
  1. At point of sale level: Greenwashing at point of sale level can happen in scenarios where:
  • Clients are advised on financial instruments, which do not meet their expressed ESG preferences;
  • Clients expect to receive sufficient information on the sustainability level of a financial instrument, but the client advisors are not sufficiently trained to provide it.

As regulators increase the scrutiny of corporate processes, it becomes more and more important to know these risks and take measures to avoid it. Therefore, it is of great importance that employees and especially front-facing staff know the topic of sustainability and also know their limits when talking to clients in order to not convene wrongful information.
