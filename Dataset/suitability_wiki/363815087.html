New justification dropdown values for "client accepts risk"

Business Critical (BC) alerts may be triggered by orders that were advised on client request (reverse solicitation) and by orders where no advice was given (unsolicited). Typical examples include buy orders in sell-rated instruments or buy orders that increase the Consolidated Product Risk beyond the upper band of the defined investment strategy. In addition, movements in the portfolio can trigger overnight alerts, even if no orders have been placed. 
When such a BC alert is triggered, the client must be informed. If the client wants to proceed (either with the order or does not want to make changes to the portfolio), there is a duty by the Bank to inform about the dedicated risks and to document that the client has accepted the according risk. 

What does "client accepts risk" mean?

In the case of a BC alert, the Bank is not allowed to provide positive advice to proceed with the transaction. Therefore, we either “advise against” - meaning we do not recommend such transactions or, for unsolicited trades, warn accordingly before the order is placed. If the client still wants to proceed, this leads to the fact that he/she has accepted the risk. This “advice against” is also noted in the “Personal Investment Recommendation” (PIR)/Suitability Statement with a dedicated warning for the client.

What will be simplified?

In addition to the above, you have been required to mention in the free text field of any resolution note or contact note (1) what risks you have communicated to the client, as well as their (2) decision, and (3) the rationale for this decision. The new justification dropdown values make this documentation now easier as you are provided with a selection of predefined rationales. However, please do not forget to add any other relevant information in the CCN to allow any third party to understand the client interaction: Advisory Session - contact note examples - Suitability Wiki - Confluence

When is free text still required?

For some justifications, additional elements are still required (e.g., client specific and individual explanations why the client accepts the risks). These requirements are outlined in the tool tips and in this article with respective examples. It is important that you familiarise yourself with these requirements, as documenting in WN is subject to regular controls and incomplete or inaccurate documenting will lead to remediation.

How do I select the new justification rationale?


  1. Select the justification “client accepts risk” if you have communicated the risk to the client and he/she acknowledged > Client's decision
  2. Select the rationale that corresponds to the client’s decision > Rationale why client decides to accept the risk
  3. Complete the free text field which is in some instances mandatory. The information button (hover over) will highlight the kind of additional information that can be noted in the field. 

Important: Text like “client wish” or “as discussed with the client” does not add any significance to the documentation and must be avoided. If text is added, please explain what you as RM explained towards the client and to which risk information the client gave her/his consent. Add any other relevant information (e.g. if you have received a mail and called the client, make sure that this is comprehensively documented). 


Note: Always first add all transactions to the session before starting to document and justify the risks (and disclosures). The snooze notification defines the validity of the justification rational and is valid until the snooze period ends or the situation worsens or improves.
