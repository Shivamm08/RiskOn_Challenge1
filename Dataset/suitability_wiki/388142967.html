What is a bank resolution?

  • A bank resolution occurs when authorities determine that a failing bank cannot go through normal insolvency proceedings without harming public interest and causing financial instability.
  • To manage the bank's failure in an orderly manner, authorities use resolution tools that ensure continuity of the bank's critical functions maintain financial stability restore the viability of parts or all of the bank
  • Meanwhile, any part of the bank that cannot be made viable again goes through normal insolvency proceedings

What is the link to the financial crisis?

  • After the financial crisis in 2008/09, the EU adopted a number of measures to harmonize and improve the tools for dealing with bank crises in its member countries

The bank resolution directive was adopted in spring 2014 to provide authorities with

-comprehensive and effective arrangements to deal with failing banks at national level

-cooperation arrangements to tackle cross-border banking failures

The directive requires banks to prepare recovery plans to overcome financial distress. It also grants national authorities powers to ensure an orderly resolution of failing banks with minimal costs for taxpayers. The directive includes rules to set up a national resolution fund that must be established by each EU country. All financial institutions have to contribute to these funds. Contributions are calculated on the basis of the institution's size and risk profile.

The EU's bank resolution rules ensure that the banks' shareholders and creditors pay their share of the costs through a "bail-in" mechanism. If that is still not sufficient, the national resolution funds set up under the BRRD can provide the resources needed to ensure that a bank can continue operating while it is being restructured. Art 44a - Selling of subordinated eligible liabilities to retail clients

A new Article 44a of BRRD I will place certain conditions on firms wishing to sell MREL-eligible liabilities to retail clients

    • Firms will have to perform and document a suitability test prior to the sale
    • The suitability test will be carried out in accordance with Article 25 of MiFID 19
    • Should a retail client have a financial instrument portfolio of less than EUR 500,000, he/she must not invest an aggregate amount exceeding 10 per cent of the portfolio in MREL eligible liabilities and that the initial investment in these liabilities is at least €10,000
    • Alternatively, EU Member States will introduce a minimum denomination of at least €50,000 for MREL eligible liabilities

In scope of the regulation are debt instruments eligible to absorb losses and to meet the minimum requirements for own funds and eligible liabilities (MREL) and total loss absorbing capacity (TLAC). The following is included in this definition:


Own funds / Regulatory capital

  • Own funds include Additional Tier 1 (AT1) and Tier 2 (T2) regulatory capital instruments.


Eligible liabilities (Senior unsecured)

Senior unsecured (non-preferred, bail-in-able) debt instruments which are subordinated to excluded liabilities' and sit at an intermediate level between common subordinated notes and ordinary unsecured notes. Three forms of debt subordination enable banks to issue senior debt that counts as loss absorbing capital:

  • contractual subordination: subordination of eligible debt to instruments that are excluded, which is determined in the debt contract
  • statutory subordination: subordination of debt is determined by the insolvency hierarchy as stipulated in the respective country’s law
  • cstructural subordination: senior debt issued by the holding companies (HoldCo) is structurally subordinated to senior debt issued by the operating companies (OpCo).


Who is in scope of the regulation? 

  1. The regulation applies directly to Julius Baer entities domiciled in the EU and the United Kingdom. All other Julius Baer entities, e.g. in Switzerland, etc., can of course decide to implement similar measures on a voluntary basis or to mitigate any risks that would arise out of business relationships with EU-domiciled clients. EU in this context covers also the EEA countries Island, Liechtenstein, Norway.
  2. The restrictions are not directly applicable to non-EEA RMLs, but following onshore distribution rules it would be consistent with the bank’s usual approach for cross-border distribution restrictions, to implement the rules globally. As a result and in agreement with the RTO cross border, the scope of the rule implementation has been defined as globally applicable
  3. Service Models: Advisory / Execution only


Which rules have been implemented for which location?

As the BRRD2 rules are based on the MiFID / FIDLEG client classification, for RMLs without a MiFID / FIDLEG client classification a ban was implemented for EEA domiciled clients. So RMs in these locations can not sell BRRD2 relevant instruments to EEA domiciled clients. 


Countries where no local law has been defined yet. For these cases the RML relevant rules will apply. 


Joint Accounts

  • For joint accounts where one AH is classified as retail and one AH is a professional, the BRRD2 rules will be applied
