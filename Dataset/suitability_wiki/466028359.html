2. Description of the alert

ESG stands for environmental (e.g. energy and water consumption), social (e.g. employer appeal and supply chain management) and governance (e.g. remuneration policy and company management). It is a set of standards and criteria used to measure a company's impact and performance, which are increasingly being framed by various laws and regulations globally.

JB classifies financial instruments into the following categories: 

  • ESG Risk
  • Traditional investment
  • Responsible investment
  • Sustainable investment

The ESG Category Mismatch alert checks if the ESG Category of the financial instrument meets the ESG preferences selected by the client in the Client Investment Profile (CIP). As this alert combines critical and non-critical elements, the different scenarios are explicitly outlined in the following.


3. What triggers this alert?

An ESG Category Mismatch alert is triggered if the financial instrument does not correspond with the ESG preferences set by the client in the CIP. This may happen

a) in the Advisory Session as a result of a newly proposed transaction, or

b) in the Client Book (non-critical only) as a result of overnight portfolio risk monitoring, e.g. due to a change of the financial instrument’s ESG categorization


4. How can this alert be resolved

You can resolve the alert with one of the following actions:


A. Advisory Session (session alert)

Solicitation Type

Duty

Documentation (Justification / Contact Note)

Actively solicited

Critical alert (exception case): Under the condition that there is no alternative/equivalent product available within the client’s ESG preferences, RM/IA can provide active advice on a single financial instrument against the client’s ESG preferences provided that the client’s approval to receive investment advice outside of his ESG preferences is received prior to providing such advice. An additional risk disclosure is in such cases printed in the PIR.  

otherwise

RM/IA must not proceed with the transaction as long as the alert is shown, because the investment advice is unsuitable

If client/order giver agrees to purchase the instrument outside his/her ESG preference: 

RM/IA to select "client accepts risk" in the alert justification and document the following in the contact note:

1) No alternative/equivalent product is available within the client’s ESG preferences

2) The client/order giver’s approval for receiving such investment advice has been obtained prior to providing the advice (please note that the client’s explicit approval is required for each trade)

3) Client/order giver’s clear acknowledgement that this investment advice will not be in line with the defined ESG preferences but he/she agrees to receive such investment advice (i.e. a one-time adaption of the ESG preferences for this trade only, no change of CIP)

Reverse solicited

Critical alert: RM/IA to inform the order giver about the ESG mismatch. RM/IA may advice the order giver outside the client’s ESG preference universe, but must inform about ESG mismatch and obtain approval before proceeding with the execution.

If client/order giver agrees to purchase the instrument outside his/her ESG preference:

RM/IA to select "client accepts risk" and document the Client/order giver’s clear acknowledgement that this investment advice will not be in line with the defined ESG preferences.  

Unsolicited

Non-critical alert: RM/IA may warn the order giver of the ESG category mismatch and correlating risks before executing the transaction

Any conversation with the client including respective warning must be duly noted in the CCN at the end of the Session.

Important: In the event of any data issues in the system or in dedicated permitted use cases (e.g. workarounds) it is possible to overrule the alert and document it according to the defined process →Hard Block Override Procedure


B. Client Book (overnight portfolio monitoring alert)

ESG category mismatches are displayed as business opportunity (non-critical) and therefore not mandatory to be followed up. However, it is an opportunity to create an investment proposal in Wealth Navigator with an instrument which matches the clients preferences and to propose a switch.

5. Justification examples

 Use Case

Examples

Session Alert

Example 1 (actively solicited)

I have contacted the client due to a critical alert (e.g. sell alert or bond rating threshold). As there is no equivalent product for the switch on the shelf for the clients ESG preferences, I asked the client for his approval to receive investment advice outside of his ESG preferences. The client agreed and acknowledged that this investment advice will not be in line with the defined ESG preferences but he/she agrees to receive such Investment Advice. I consequently suggested a switch from A to B. 


Example 2 (reverse solicited)

The client called me and asked for advice on instrument A (specific instrument). According to our systems, instrument A is a Traditional Investment, however, the client has selected a Responsible ESG preference in his/her CIP. I informed the client about the ESG preference mismatch. The client acknowledged the mismatch but nevertheless wanted to proceed with the transaction.
