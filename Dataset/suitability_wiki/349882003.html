JB Climate Score

https://wiki.juliusbaer.com/spaces/fkh/pages/349882003/JB+Climate+Score

In short:

The JB Climate score addresses the question of greenhouse gas emissions and a company's exposure to the shift towards a net-zero world. The climate theme allows to identify and manage climate exposure. The score considers greenhouse gas emissions per sales (in USD Millions) which are directly attributable to the issuer. This includes direct emissions from the production process or from the energy usage of the company. Emissions stemming from the value-chain of an issuer are not considered in this score.

Short explanation of the calculation:

For the status quo element of the JB Climate score, reported data on emissions and revenue streams are used. The base is the corporate issuer’s scope 1 and 2 emissions, which are mapped to a JB score. In case the corporate issuer is active in the fossil fuel industry and >1% of revenue is derived from coal, or >10% of revenue is derived from oil and gas, a -3 score is assigned. Accordingly, the EU Taxonomy aligned revenue data is used to reward corporate issuers that are already actively contributing to climate change adaptation and mitigation. In case the corporate issuer has Taxonomy aligned revenue, the taxonomy aligned based score is averaged with the base score based on the scope 1 & 2 intensity or fossil fuel base score.


To the base a risk adjustment is made in case a climate related risk score from Sustainalytics indicates the corporate issuer is at high risk14. Also, a PAI downgrade is applied for corporate issuers with a high scope 3 emissions intensity (top 10% scope 3 emission intensity of the MSCI world).

In the transition element of the climate score SBTi approved targets are used to identify corporate issuers that are committed to make their operations climate neutral. EU Taxonomy aligned Capex and the NG leadership statuses are used to identify those corporate issuers that are working on solutions and products for a sustainable future.


To ensure regulatory compliance, an upper limit is applied to ensure that the climate score of corporate issuers with coal, oil and gas revenues are limited to at a maximum a

Responsible Investment classification. This ensures that corporate issuers with fossil fuel exposure are not classified as Sustainable Investments.
