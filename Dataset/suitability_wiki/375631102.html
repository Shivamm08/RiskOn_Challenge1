1. For which Service Models is this alert applicable?

Please find all information in the following chapter: →Alerts configuration per Advisory Location


2. Description of the alert

Certain countries have imposed a ban or restriction on Digital Assets. The ban is valid for Direct Digital Assets (Crypto Tokens) and Indirect Digital Assets (Structured Products, Exchange Traded Products & Exchange Traded Funds with Digital Assets underlying). Exchange Traded Products with Digital Assets underlying are collateralized debt notes with return linked to an underlying Crypto Token or a basket of Crypto Tokens.

The alert checks if the account holder is from a banned domicile or the British Virgin Islands (BVI). Currently China and India are banned domiciles (please also see →Digital Assets Banned Domiciles).


3. What triggers this alert?

This alert is triggered in the Advisory Session as a result of a newly proposed transaction for Direct and Indirect Digital Assets if the account holder is from 

  • a banned domicile or
  • the British Virgin Islands (BVI) 

It applies for “Actively solicited”, “Reverse solicited” and “Unsolicited” orders.

Exception: The alert is not triggered for unsolicited orders of Indirect Digital Assets if the account holder is domiciled in China or in the BVI.

You can resolve the alert with one of the following actions:

RM/IA to inform the client that this is a hard block and client cannot invest.

If the account holder is from the BVI

The RM/IA must verify if the beneficial owner’s domicile. If this domicile is not China, the RM/IA may justify and proceed with the transaction.

If the account holder is from a banned domicile

No justification possible (as this is a hard block)                                                                   

If the account holder is from the BVI

RM/IA may select “Wrong underlying data” in the alert justification and document that the beneficial owner is not domiciled in China.

Important: In the event of any other data issues in the system or in dedicated permitted use cases (e.g. workarounds) it is possible to overrule the alert and document it according to the defined process →Hard Block Override Procedure
