1. Do best execution requirements apply when executing or transmitting orders for execution for eligible counterparties?


No. This is a great example where we can see that eligible counterparties are a category of clients who have waived as much investor protection as possible: when executing or transmitting orders for execution given by eligible counterparties, BJBE is not obliged to comply with the arrangements laid down in the internal best execution policy.

2. Can the update of the K&E form and the CIP be done through e-signature?2


Yes.

3. If the client confirms that their CIP remains unchanged when I am in the process of renewing it following its expiration, can I simply confirm it?3


Yes. As per LUX-P-1275-02 Client Investment Profile, if the client has confirmed that their CIP remains unchanged, then you have to renew it on TAP using the option “renewal without change” and the validity of the existing CIP will get extended for 2 more years and this will automatically be documented in a TAP contact note.

4. Can a retail client request to be treated as per se professional?


As explained in the training:


  • Per se professional clients can only be legal entities, but never individuals;


  • A client qualifies as per se professional because of their:

Nature: because they are licensed to operate in the financial markets (e.g. they are an investment firm, a bank, an insurance company, a fund, a fund ManCo, etc), because they are national or regional governments, central banks, other type of institutional investor, etc.; or

Financial situation: they are a large undertaking meeting at least 2 of the following 3 conditions: (i) they have a balance sheet total of at least EUR 20 million, (ii) they have a net turnover of at least EUR 40 million, (iii) they have own funds worth at least EUR 2 million.


More details in the Client Categorisation Q&As; and


  • Clients are usually immediately categorised as per se professional at on-boarding because it is detected at that time that they meet the criteria indicated in the bullet point above. However, for example, it may happen that their eligibility to be treated as per se professional due to their financial situation (see bullet point above) is not detected by BJBE at on-boarding and that then the client, categorised as retail, requests to be treated as per se professional. In such a case, an assessment of whether they meet the indicated criteria will be performed and, if met, they will be re-categorised.

5. What if, as part of the CIP renewal process, my client says that only one of the answers to the questions in the CIP has changed but this answer has no impact in the score of their overall financial risk ability or overall risk tolerance (and hence of their overall risk profile)? In such a case, can I simply renew the CIP on TAP “without changes” making reference to the slight, impact-less change mentioned by the client in a TAP contact note?


No. As per the current version of LUX-P-1275-02 Client Investment Profile, “if the client communicates to the RM that his situation has changed, a new CIP must be completed and signed by the client”. This is a rule we have to adhere to because it is part of our internal policy framework, compliance with which will be monitored by Front Risk Management in the first line of defence, Risk Management and Compliance in the second line of defence, internal audit in the third line of defence, external auditors and, if needed, the CSSF as part of its supervisory tasks.


However, we are committed to hear and take into account your suggestions. The internal policy framework can be amended as long as it does not contravene legal and regulatory requirements and remains consistent. The situation described in this question certainly calls of a review of the mentioned internal rule. Hence we (Front Risk Management and Compliance as owners of the procedure) will align internally and revert to you if we change it.

6. Are private equity funds legally restricted to retail clients?


Private equity funds qualify as alternative investment funds (AIFs). Distribution of AIFs can be carried out through marketing or on a purely non-advised scenario. As for marketing, article 43 of the AIFMD leaves it up to each European Union (EU) Member State to decide whether or not they allow market AIFs to retail clients. ESMA reported that, as of 30 June 2021, 25 out of 27 EU Member States allowed it, but under certain conditions. Hence, it is not that distributing AIFs to retail clients is legally prohibited (at least in most EU countries) – it is just that it is quite complicated!


Because of this, the Julius Baer Group (including BJBE), for the time being, follows a conservative approach: it restricts the sale of private equity funds to retail clients. Because of this, for a client to have a chance to subscribe a private equity fund, they have to be categorised at least as “elective professional for private equity investments”. Of course, as said during the training, that is not enough. Eligibility for a client to purchase any financial instrument derives from that purchase complying with (1) the financial instrument’s own rules and selling restrictions, (2) the applicable legal and regulatory framework and (3) our internal policy framework.


Hence the restriction of private equity funds to retail clients actually comes mainly from our internal policy framework..

9. Say I have a Webex call with the client (that qualifies as a face-to-face meeting that I must document). I live in a neighbouring country (Germany, France or Belgium) but our office is in Luxembourg. In the DiAS contact note, (1) do I have to write my own home address as the “location of the meeting” or that of our office in Luxembourg and (2) do I have to select “cross border activity” = “yes” or “no”?


As clarified by Legal, Webex calls do not qualify as “meetings” taking place in one location but as “calls” where the interlocutors are at different locations. The locations of both interlocutors of the Webex call (RM and client) have to be observed. Therefore, the location of the RM is the location where the RM is taking part at the Webex call (e.g. the RM’s domicile). The Webex call has to be classified as cross-border activity as soon as the client is not in the same country as the RM or in  Luxembourg where BJBE is located. Accordingly, a cross-border Webex call needs to comply with all cross-border rules.

10. My client is categorised as professional. Does that mean they are eligible to buy US ETFs?


No. Just because your client is categorised as professional doesn’t mean that they are automatically eligible to buy US ETFs.


US ETFs qualify as non-EU domiciled alternative investment funds. To be able to distribute US ETFs, we (you and your local Compliance) must ensure compliance with:


(1) The US ETF’s own rules (i.e. does its prospectus allow the distribution to the client in question?); 


(2) The applicable legal and regulatory framework (i.e. do the AIFMD and MiFID II (mainly) allow the distribution to the client in question?);


=> A key question here will be whether the US ETF has been registered for marketing in the country where your client is domiciled:


> If yes, you will (in principle, though you will need to check step 3 below first) be able to advise your client to subscribe it. Your client will also (in principle, though you will need to check step 3 below first) be able to give you a non-advised order to subscribe it;


> If no, you will (in principle, though you will need to check step 3 below first) only be able to distribute it on a non-advised basis which you will have to document clearly – you must create a TAP contact note, indicating:


If email, attach the email where the order was given to the TAP contact note;

If face-to-face meeting, (i) date and time of the meeting, (ii) location of the meeting, (iii) identity of the attendees, (iv) initiator of the meeting, (v) summary of the meeting, including at least any relevant information about the client including the price, volume, type of order and when it shall be transmitted or executed;

If phone, indicate (i) who called who, (ii) the date and time of the call, and (iii) from your perspective, whether it was a landline / cell phone line conversation; and


> Where you were and the client was when you received the order (if the client is a legal entity, then it is the country of domicile of the legal entity client).


(3) Our internal policy framework (i.e. does the the LUX-P-1276-06 Solicitation Guidelines procedure (mainly) allow the distribution to the client in question?)
