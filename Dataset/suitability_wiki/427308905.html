2. What is the regulatory requirement?

In short, under MiFID2, products have manufacturers and distributors.

  • On the one hand, manufacturers must define the potential target market (positive and negative) as well as the distribution strategy for the product.
  • On the other hand, prior to distributing the product, distributors must identify the actual target market (positive and negative) within their own client base, and then follow the defined distribution strategy when distributing it.

That definition and identification of the positive, out of positive and negative target market must be done:

  • In terms of 5 categories: (i) category of the client, (ii) knowledge and experience, (iii) financial situation (capacity to bear losses), (iv) risk tolerance, (v) objectives and needs (including ESG preferences, time horizon, etc.).
  • Based on (a) the risk and complexity of the financial instrument and (b) the distribution strategy.

As product distributor, Julius Baer must identify whether clients in its client base are in the positive, out of positive or negative target market of each product it distributes.


3. What is the Target Market test?

The way Julius Baer entities subject to MiFID2 do this is by having DiAS, automatically and pre-BUY-trade1, perform the Target Market test, which is divided up into three checks:

  1. Manufacturer Target Market (‘MTM’): product data with the manufacturer’s definition of the positive target market2 is compared against client data to determine whether the client is part of the positive or out of positive target market.
  2. Negative Manufacturer Target Market (‘-MTM’): product data with the manufacturer’s definition of the negative target market2 is compared against client data to determine whether the client is part of the negative target market.
  3. Distributor Target Market (‘DTM’): based on the asset class that the product has been recorded in the internal systems as belonging to as well as on client data, the client is determined to be either in the positive or out of positive target market. 3

1 When Julius Baer distributes a product, from the client’s perspective, it is always a BUY trade (never a SELL one). 

2 Received by Julius Baer from external data provider “WM Daten” (for all asset classes except structured products) and then recorded in FMA, or manually entered in SPACE by Markets in Bank Julius Baer & Co. Ltd. (for structured products) and then recorded in FMA. This information is also generally available on PSP. Generally, such data is only available in relation to EEA-manufactured products because only manufacturers domiciled in the EEA are subject to MiFID2.

3 Because Julius Baer also distributes non-EEA-manufactured products and, when doing so, Julius Baer entities subject to MiFID2 must still ensure that the product reaches its identified target market, the DTM check is performed.

4. What is a Target Market alert?

DiAS runs all three checks (#1 MTM, #2 -MTM, and #3 DTM) in parallel, and keeps the worst-of result between checks #1, #2 and #3.

If it detects that the client is part of the positive target market, DiAS triggers no alert.

However, whenever DiAS detects that the client is part of the out of positive or negative target market, it triggers an alert as follows:

On the one hand:

  • The S’s on the matrixes mean that the client is considered to be either in the out of positive or negative target market – however, the distribution of the product can still go ahead as long as it is adequately justified.
  • The H’s on the matrixes mean that the client is considered to be either in the out of positive or negative target market - and the distribution of the product cannot take place, unless there is a valid “data exception” which is justified and documented.

On the other hand, the “Y’s” on the matrixes mean that the client is considered to be in the positive target market. In such a case, DiAS triggers no alert.


5. How do Target Market alerts have to be handled?

The Front Officer must justify the alert with one of the following “justification options” in order to proceed with the distribution of the product:

  • Soft blocks
    • “Diversification”
    • “Hedging”
    • “Client’s sophistication”
    • “Client’s financial situation”
    • “Local market differences”
    • “Data exception”
  • Hard blocks
    • “Data exception”

6. Is the client notified that they are outside or even in the negative target market of the product in any case?

Yes. In an “actively solicited” and “reverse solicited” scenario, the identified Target Market deviation as well as the justification of the alert done by the Front Officer will be indicated in the Personal Investment Recommendation (“PIR”) which DiAS will automatically generate and sent pre-trade to the client.

However, in an “unsolicited” scenario, the client not notified when they are outside or even in the negative target market of the product.


7. Why is it crucial that the Front Office handles Target Market alerts diligently?

The Front Office must diligently handle Target Market alerts because Julius Baer entities subject to MiFID2 must:

  • Do their best to ensure that products reach their defined positive target market;
  • Review whether they distributed products to their positive target market. In this sense, a Target Market Dashboard allows the respective Instrument Class Owners (“ICOs”) to do a life-cycle management review of the identified positive, out of positive and negative target market of their products; and
  • Report to manufacturers cases where products were distributed to their outside positive or even negative target market, so that manufacturers can, if necessary, refine their definition of the positive and negative target market.

This reporting is done proactively once per year in Q1 of Year+1, as follows:

  • It is done only to relevant manufacturers, i.e. manufacturers meeting the following criteria:
  1. The manufacturer is domiciled in the EEA or UK.
  2. Data in relation to the manufacturer’s definition of the positive and negative target market has been received and recorded in FMA.
  3. Julius Baer has a distribution agreement with the manufacturer.

A list of relevant manufacturers is kept up to date by Fund Provider Management team and Legal Markets team at BJB CH.

  • Based on trading data and the aforementioned list and with a defined letter template, an ad hoc letter is prepared for each relevant manufacturer, including “total tales”, “total sales outside the positive target market (and % over total sales)” and “total sales into the negative target market (category deviated) (and % over total sales)”.

Each ad hoc letter is reviewed by the respective Instrument Class Owner (“ICO”) before it is finally sent to the manufacturer.
